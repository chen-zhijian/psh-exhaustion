# Changes in Version 4

**5 November 2026** · [Version 4](papers/paper_v4.pdf) · [Version 3](papers/paper_v3.pdf)

The paper is now titled **Regularity of plurisubharmonic exhaustions and related problems**. Version 4 reorganizes the introduction, clarifies terminology and historical references, and revises the exposition throughout Sections 2–6. The principal conclusions remain the threefold counterexamples, the regularity criterion for line bundles, surface regularization, and the curve-fibering criterion for holomorphic convexity.

## Introduction and applications

The introduction begins with continuous plurisubharmonic exhaustions and Nakano's question about replacing smoothness by continuity. It uses **weakly 1-complete** and **weakly pseudoconvex** for spaces admitting a smooth psh exhaustion, and explains Demailly's broader definition. On a Kähler manifold, a continuous psh exhaustion already provides the complete Kähler metric used in his $L^2$ estimates for solving $\bar\partial$.

A **Main Results** subsection introduces the theorems. Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2 use the regularity notation $C^{0,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ and $C^{1,0}$, followed by an explanation of the convention. Notation is introduced where it is used, and the applications appear under numbered, bold titles.

The discussion now connects the Grauert metric obstruction to the threefold construction, explains the Hopf-surface product and modification with a compact center, and cites Hironaka's opposite-order blowups. It motivates the surface results, defines the pluriharmonic logarithmic collar, and explains the relation between the curve-fibering theorem and Ohsawa's surface theorem. References to Demailly, Hironaka's construction, Grauert's criterion, and the earlier questions are made explicit.

## Statements and proofs

- Section 2 is titled **Distinguished line bundles on Grauert's ruled surface**, and Section 3 is titled **The regularity criterion**. Their exposition clarifies the extension cocycle, first-normal derivative argument, metric conventions, and global Lipschitz metric construction.
- Theorem 3.4 states its two equivalent conditions as **(i)** and **(ii)**, with matching proof directions. The averaging and radius-recovery arguments are streamlined while retaining the estimates for Hölder and real analytic regularity.
- The modification argument and the local coordinates for the Hironaka construction are made more direct.
- Proposition 5.3 presents three equivalent conditions. Its proof uses the regularity criterion to recover nonnegative degree and constructs the converse metric on the compact curve.
- The surface arguments explain the boundary collar, level-current averaging, and numerical cases with consistent notation and explicit transitions. The text connects the surface theorem in Section 5 to Theorem 1.3.
- Theorem 6.1 is explicitly identified as **(=Theorem 1.4)**. Its proof clarifies the fiber-cycle argument, uniform area bounds, and the exhaustion used in the local convexity construction.

Redundant scope qualifications and repeated explanations are removed throughout the manuscript. Necessary hypotheses and estimates remain in the theorem statements and proofs.
