# Open directions and limits

These directions are extracted from the supplied revised [manuscript](../preprints/001-psh-exhaustion/psh_exhaustion_refereed.pdf). “Unresolved here” describes the manuscript's scope, not the outcome of a fresh exhaustive literature search.

## Regularity between locally Lipschitz and $C^2$

Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 provide locally Lipschitz psh exhaustions but exclude every $C^2$ psh exhaustion on the constructed threefolds. They do not determine whether those manifolds admit a $C^1$ psh exhaustion. The Lipschitz endpoint of the constructed cusp is not asserted to be an optimal regularity threshold.

The radius criterion is proved for $C^2$ and smooth exhaustions. Extending it to lower regularity, or finding a different obstruction at that regularity, would require a separate argument.

## The unrestricted surface problem

The manuscript leaves unresolved whether every complex surface admitting a continuous psh exhaustion admits a $C^2$ one. It does not settle all Kähler surfaces or all smooth quasi-projective surfaces. Its dimension-three construction therefore does not prove that three is the smallest possible counterexample dimension.

Proposition 5.1 handles surfaces with a nonconstant holomorphic function by an existing holomorphic-convexity theorem. The remaining case $\mathcal O(X)=\mathbb C$ is not automatically covered. Proposition 5.2 excludes a counterexample formed as the dual total space of a line bundle over a compact curve, but this is only one possible construction.

## Residual divisor complements

For projective compactifications, Corollary 5.7 gives necessary conditions for a hypothetical continuous-but-not-smooth example. After boundary resolution, the boundary must be connected and support an effective nef divisor $A$ such that

$$
A\cdot D_i=0,\qquad A^2=0,\qquad
\kappa(S,A)=0,\qquad K_S\cdot A\ge0.
$$

These conditions neither produce a continuous psh exhaustion nor rule out a smooth one. They describe candidates not eliminated by the preceding arguments.

For a smooth connected square-zero boundary curve in a projective surface, a candidate must also avoid every sufficient condition in Proposition 5.8. In particular, the manuscript requires genus at least two, $q(S)<g(C)$, infinite Ueda type without a unitary flat neighborhood, and infinite image of the relevant normal-bundle character on the kernel of the homology map.

The analytic question is whether a continuous divergent psh collar in such a neighborhood forces a smooth divergent collar, or whether a counterexample can be constructed. **Infinite Ueda type is a formal condition; it does not by itself supply convergent unitary defining functions.**

## Compactification and classification limits

Theorems 5.4 and 5.5 require their stated compactifications. An actual smooth ruling is more restrictive than birational ruledness. A blowup at a boundary point preserves the open complement only when the entire reduced inverse image is retained as boundary. A general blowdown may alter the open manifold.

Consequently, Table 1 does not reduce the unrestricted surface problem to “only Kodaira dimensions one and two.” It classifies conclusions for the stated minimal compactifier pairs, not arbitrary open surfaces. The projective elliptic case still has horizontal-boundary configurations outside the listed criteria, and the general-type case retains connected rigid square-zero candidates.

## Bounded potentials beyond the specified example

Remark 2.5 supplies locally Lipschitz bounded potentials for the particular modified Grauert line bundle used in Theorem 1.1. The manuscript distinguishes this from Grauert's original example, which it does not identify with that bundle or resolve. It also makes no claim that every nef and big line bundle has such a metric, or that every representative with minimal singularities has continuous potentials.
