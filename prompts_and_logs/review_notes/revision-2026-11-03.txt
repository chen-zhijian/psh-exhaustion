Integration and principal changes

The revision integrates the supplied grauert-regularity.pdf and
real-analytic-radius-criterion.pdf into the manuscript. The supporting
notes and their available sources are preserved separately as revision
inputs; the integrated manuscript gives the current statements and proofs.

The introductory threefold theorem states existence of a connected smooth
quasi-projective Kähler threefold with a locally Lipschitz psh exhaustion
and no C^1 psh exhaustion. Its construction and proof appear in the body.
The non-Kähler example retains the same exhaustion regularity gap.

The ruled-surface argument uses nonzero extensions of the trivial line
bundle by a positive-degree line bundle. It proves the C^1 obstruction by
restriction to holomorphic graphs and a resulting splitting contradiction.
The quantitative cusp construction and metric gluing provide locally
Lipschitz semipositive metrics, smooth with strictly positive curvature off
the negative section.

The former Corollary 3.4 is Theorem 3.4, “Regularity criterion.” For a line
bundle over a compact complex manifold, existence of a semipositive metric
is equivalent to existence of a psh exhaustion on the dual total space in
each of the following classes: C^r for nonnegative integer r or r=infinity;
local C^{k,alpha} for nonnegative integer k and 0<alpha<=1; and real analytic.
No Kähler or projectivity hypothesis on the base is imposed.

Mathematical audit

The C^1 proof explicitly fixes a compact test disc and a uniform normal
tube before choosing the graph parameter. It obtains smoothness from
harmonicity of the real and imaginary parts before differentiating the
normal coefficient. The cusp construction states the psh removability
argument at the divisor.

The regularity proof retains compact-group averaging, a common complex
polydisc and uniform factorial estimates for analytic averaging, convex
radial profiles, a holomorphic-disc proof of positivity, the inverse
secant estimate for local Hölder data, and implicit-function arguments for
higher and real analytic regularity. The Hölder bounds use one constant
on a compact base-and-radius cylinder, including the Lipschitz endpoint.
The proof distinguishes C^{k,1} from C^{k+1}.

The modification, surface, and curve-fibering arguments were also reviewed.
The surface theorem and its general noncompact-surface limitation remain
as stated. The curve-fibering proof retains its assumptions on every fiber
and the detailed support and uniform-area arguments for singular spaces.
The review found no remaining gap requiring withdrawal or weakening of
the revised principal results. This records the review findings, not an
independent formal verification of the mathematics.

Enoki source check

The file supplied as Enoki1980.pdf contains Enoki's article published in
Tohoku Mathematical Journal 33 (1981), pages 453–492; the 1980 dates concern
receipt and revision. The bibliography correctly uses 1981.

The source check covered the introductory classification on page 453,
the Main Theorem on page 454, the affine-bundle degree convention in
Section 2 on page 457, and Proposition 3.5(iii) on page 460. The argument
now explicitly records A_0=m C_E with m>0 and supp A_0=C_E, and identifies
the complement as an affine-line bundle with linearization Q of degree -n.
In reciprocal fiber coordinates, the infinity section has normal bundle
Q^{-1}; its self-intersection is n>0. This supplies the positive boundary
collar used in the exhaustion argument. The supplied third-party PDF is
not included in this repository; the article is cited in the manuscript.
