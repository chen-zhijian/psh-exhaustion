\begin{abstract}
We construct K\"ahler and non-K\"ahler threefolds with locally Lipschitz
psh exhaustions but no $C^2$ psh exhaustion; the K\"ahler example is
quasi-projective. We prove smooth regularization for divisor complements
in compact K\"ahler surfaces and give a holomorphic convexity criterion
for weakly complete spaces fibered by curves over affine space.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and main results}\label{sec:intro}

A continuous plurisubharmonic exhaustion need not admit a smooth
replacement, even on a quasi-projective manifold. We construct
threefold examples and prove affirmative results for compactifiable
surfaces and spaces fibered by curves. Throughout, psh means
plurisubharmonic and smooth means $C^\infty$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Let $C$ be the smooth projective completion of $y^2=x^5-x$, let
$K=K_C$, and choose a nonsplit extension
\begin{equation}\label{eq:extension-intro}
0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow\OO_C
\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-line}
\begin{gathered}
S=\PP_{\mathrm{lines}}(V),\quad \pi:S\to C,\quad Y=\PP(K),\\
L=\OO_S(Y)\otimes\pi^*K,\qquad M=\Tot(L^{-1}).
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Then $S$ is projective and $M$ is a smooth quasi-projective K\"ahler
threefold. The line bundle $L$ admits a locally Lipschitz Hermitian
metric with psh weights, but no $C^2$ semipositive Hermitian metric.
The threefold $M$ admits a locally Lipschitz psh exhaustion, but no
$C^2$ psh exhaustion.
\end{theorem}

The ruled surface is Koike's modified Grauert example
\cite[Example~4.1]{Koike2021}; the semipositivity obstruction is known
\cite[Theorem~1.4]{Koike2021}\cite[Remark~2.8]{Wu2026}.
Our proof supplies an explicit continuous metric and uses the radius
criterion (Corollary~\ref{cor:criterion}): for a line bundle $A$ over
a compact complex manifold and $r\in\{2,\infty\}$,
\[
\begin{gathered}
\Tot(A^{-1})\text{ has a }C^r\text{ psh exhaustion}
\\\Longleftrightarrow\\
A\text{ has a }C^r\text{ semipositive metric}.
\end{gathered}
\]
The obstruction therefore applies to every $C^2$ exhaustion. The
$C^1$ threshold remains undetermined.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:nonkahler-threefold}
For every $x\in M$ there is a smooth non-K\"ahler threefold $N$ and a
proper holomorphic modification $\mu:N\to M$ such that
\[
N\setminus\mu^{-1}(x)\simeq M\setminus\{x\}.
\]
The threefold $N$ admits a locally Lipschitz psh exhaustion, but no
$C^2$ psh exhaustion.
\end{theorem}

Hironaka's opposite-order blowups produce the non-K\"ahler
configuration over $x$. Proposition~\ref{prop:compact-modification}
transfers the exhaustion properties through this compactly supported
modification.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:surface-introduction}
Let $S$ be a smooth compact connected K\"ahler surface and $D\ne0$
an effective divisor. If $S\setminus\supp D$ admits a continuous
psh exhaustion, it admits a smooth one. Consequently, the same
implication holds for every smooth complex quasi-projective surface.
\end{theorem}

The proof averages positive $dd^c$-closed currents over exhaustion
levels to obtain a current with zero Lelong numbers. The current
theorem of Dinh--Nguy\^en--Sibony \cite{DNS22} and Hodge index then
give the following alternative: either $S\setminus\supp D$ is a
modification of a Stein space, or a divisor
$A=\sum a_iD_i$, $a_i\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$, satisfies
\[
\supp A=\supp D,\qquad A^2=0,\qquad
\OO_S(A)\text{ is unitary flat near }D.
\]
Its canonical section yields a pluriharmonic logarithmic collar.
Section~\ref{sec:surfaces} also gives separate numerical and
neighborhood criteria for existence of an exhaustion.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:curve-fiber-introduction}
Let $X$ be a reduced complex space of pure dimension $n\ge2$ with a
continuous real-valued psh exhaustion. If a holomorphic map
\[
F:X\longrightarrow\C^{n-1}
\]
has every nonempty fiber of dimension at most one, then $X$ is
holomorphically convex and admits a smooth psh exhaustion.
\end{theorem}

The fiber bound includes exceptional fibers. Analytic fiber cycles
and Bishop compactness establish
the stability needed for V\^{\i}j\^{\i}itu's convexity criteria
\cite{VijituForum2022}. For irreducible surfaces, convexity already
follows from \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Vajaitu2022}.

Regularization here means existence of a smooth exhaustion. The
problem for arbitrary noncompact complex surfaces remains open. Within smooth
quasi-projective manifolds, Theorems~\ref{thm:main}
and~\ref{thm:surface-introduction} place the least possible
counterexample dimension at three.

\paragraph{Conventions.}\label{sec:conventions}
We use
\[
d^c=\frac{i}{4\pi}(\barp-\partial),\qquad
\ddc=\frac{i}{2\pi}\partial\barp,\qquad
\|e\|_h^2=e^{-\varphi},\qquad\Theta_h(A)=\ddc\varphi.
\]
For local frames $e_j=g_{ij}e_i$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:metric-transition}
\varphi_j=\varphi_i-\log|g_{ij}|^2.
\end{equation}
A continuous Hermitian metric is semipositive when its weights are
psh. For $C^2$ functions, write
$\Levi_f(\xi)=\sum f_{z_j\bar z_k}\xi_j\overline{\xi_k}$.
For the canonical section $s_D$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:PL}
\ddc\log\|s_D\|_h^2=[D]-\Theta_h(\OO(D)).
\end{equation}
An exhaustion is a continuous real-valued function with compact
sublevel sets. Continuous or smooth weak completeness means existence
of a psh exhaustion of that regularity. On complex spaces, smoothness
means local restriction of smooth ambient functions.
The projectivization $\PP_{\mathrm{lines}}(E)$ parametrizes lines,
with tautological bundle $\OO_{\PP(E)}(-1)$.

\section{The ruled surface and its metrics}\label{sec:metrics}

\subsection{Geometry and the first-order obstruction}\label{sec:ruled}

The map $C\to\PP^1$ defined by $y^2=x^5-x$ has six simple branch
points, including infinity. Hence
\[
 2g(C)-2=-4+6=2,\qquad \deg K=2,\qquad
 \operatorname{Ext}^1(\OO_C,K)=H^1(C,K)\simeq\C.
\]
Choose a nonzero extension class as in \eqref{eq:extension-intro}.
All such classes give isomorphic middle terms after scalar
automorphisms of the end terms. By GAGA, $V$ is algebraic; thus
$S=\PP_{\mathrm{lines}}(V)$ is projective. For $Y=\PP(K)$ and
$L=\OO_S(Y)\otimes\pi^*K$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:restriction}
 N_{Y/S}\simeq\operatorname{Hom}(K,V/K)\simeq K^{-1},\qquad
 Y^2=-2,\qquad L|_Y\simeq\OO_Y.
\end{equation}
The normal-bundle identification sends the derivative of a varying
line through $K_z\subset V_z$ to its linear map
$K_z\to V_z/K_z$. The last trivialization in
\eqref{eq:restriction} is the evaluation map
$K^{-1}\otimes K\to\OO_C$. Since
$Y\cdot\pi^*K=\deg K=2$ and $(\pi^*K)^2=0$, one has
\[
 L^2=Y^2+2Y\cdot\pi^*K+(\pi^*K)^2=-2+4=2.
\]
If $F$ is a fiber and $D\ne Y$ is an irreducible horizontal curve,
\[
 L\cdot Y=0,\quad L\cdot F=1,\quad L^2=2,\quad
 L\cdot D=Y\cdot D+2\deg(\pi|_D)>0.
\]
Thus $L$ is nef and big. This is the modified Grauert example of
\cite[Example~4.1]{Koike2021}.

Choose local frames $k_i$ of $K$ and lifts $v_i$ of $1\in\OO_C$,
with
\[
 k_j=g_{ij}k_i,\qquad v_j=v_i+c_{ij}k_i.
\]
Use the coordinate maps
\[
 (z,w_i)\longmapsto [k_i(z)+w_iv_i(z)]\in S
\]
near $Y$. Equality of the lines represented in charts $i,j$ means
\[
 k_i+w_iv_i=\lambda\bigl((g_{ij}+c_{ij}w_j)k_i+w_jv_i\bigr).
\]
Comparing coefficients gives
$w_i=w_j/(g_{ij}+c_{ij}w_j)$. Let $d_i$ be the frame of
$\OO_S(Y)$ determined by $s_Y=w_id_i$, and put
$\ell_i=d_i\otimes\pi^*k_i$. Since $w_id_i=w_jd_j$,
the coordinate and frame changes are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L-transition}
 w_j=\frac{g_{ij}w_i}{1-c_{ij}w_i},\qquad
 \ell_j=(1-c_{ij}w_i)\ell_i.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:no-metric}
The line bundle $L$ has no $C^2$ semipositive Hermitian metric.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Suppose its local weights $\varphi_i$ are $C^2$ and psh.
The frame changes in \eqref{eq:L-transition} equal $1$ on $Y$,
so $\varphi_i|_Y$ define a global subharmonic function on the
compact connected curve $Y$. This function is constant; rescale the
metric to obtain $\varphi_i(z,0)=0$. At $(z,0)$ the Levi matrix is
positive semidefinite, and therefore
\[
 |\varphi_{i,w\bar z}(z,0)|^2
 \le \varphi_{i,z\bar z}(z,0)\varphi_{i,w\bar w}(z,0)=0.
\]
Consequently the $C^1$ function $a_i(z)=\varphi_{i,w}(z,0)$
satisfies $\bar\partial a_i=0$ and is holomorphic. The metric
transition law is
\[
 \varphi_j(z,w_j)=\varphi_i(z,w_i)-\log|1-c_{ij}w_i|^2.
\]
Differentiate with respect to $w_i$ at $w_i=0$. Since
$\partial w_j/\partial w_i=g_{ij}$ there, this gives
\[
 g_{ij}a_j=a_i+c_{ij},\qquad
 v_j-a_jk_j=v_i+(c_{ij}-g_{ij}a_j)k_i=v_i-a_ik_i.
\]
Thus the maps
\[
 s_i:\OO_C|_{U_i}\longrightarrow V|_{U_i},\qquad
 f\longmapsto f(v_i-a_ik_i)
\]
glue to a holomorphic map $s:\OO_C\to V$. Its composition with
$V\to\OO_C$ is the identity, contradicting the nonsplitting of
\eqref{eq:extension-intro}. This is the first-order obstruction of
\cite[Theorem~1.4]{Koike2021}; the calculation uses only $C^2$
regularity.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cusp correction and a global metric}\label{sec:cusp}

\begin{lemma}[Cusp correction]\label{lem:cusp}
Let $D$ be a compact smooth curve in a complex surface, with
$\deg N_{D/X}<0$, and let $A|_D$ be unitary flat. Choose smooth
metrics $h_0$ on $A$ and $h_D$ on $\OO_X(D)$ such that $h_0|_D$ is
flat and $\Theta_{h_D}|_{TD}<0$. Put $q=\|s_D\|_{h_D}^2$.
For $0<a\le\tfrac12$, the local weights
\[
 \varphi_0+\Lambda q^a
\]
are psh near $D$, strictly psh off $D$, and locally $C^{0,2a}$,
for a suitable $\Lambda>0$. One may take $\Lambda=1$ when
$a<\tfrac12$; a sufficiently large $\Lambda$ works when
$a=\tfrac12$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Negative degree gives a negative curvature metric on
$N_{D/X}=\OO_X(D)|_D$. To extend a prescribed metric from $D$,
fix any smooth metric $\widehat h$ on the ambient line bundle,
write the prescribed metric as $\widehat h|_D e^{-f}$, extend the
smooth real function $f$ to a neighborhood, and use
$\widehat h e^{-\widetilde f}$. This constructs both required metrics.
In a finite coordinate cover of $D$, write
\[
 D=\{w=0\},\quad q=|w|^2e^{\beta(z,w)},\quad
 \beta_{z\bar z}(z,0)>0,\quad \tau=\log q.
\]
For $w\ne0$ and $v=(\xi,w\nu)$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:scaled-cusp}
 q^{-a}\Levi_{q^a}(v)
 &=a\Levi_\tau(v)+a^2|\partial\tau(v)|^2\notag\\
 &=a\beta_{z\bar z}(z,0)|\xi|^2
   +a^2|\nu+\beta_z(z,0)\xi|^2
   +O(|w|)(|\xi|^2+|\nu|^2).
\end{align}
The terms in \eqref{eq:scaled-cusp} follow from
\[
 \begin{split}
 \partial\tau(\xi,w\nu)
 &=\beta_z\xi+(1+w\beta_w)\nu,\\
 \Levi_\tau(\xi,w\nu)
 &=\beta_{z\bar z}|\xi|^2
   +2\operatorname{Re}(\beta_{z\bar w}\xi\overline{w\nu})
   +\beta_{w\bar w}|w\nu|^2.
 \end{split}
\]
The leading form in \eqref{eq:scaled-cusp} is positive definite:
its vanishing first forces $\xi=0$, and then $\nu=0$.
On a finite cover by relatively compact coordinate sets, its smallest
eigenvalue has a positive lower bound. Bound the remainder by
$C_1|w|(|\xi|^2+|\nu|^2)$ and shrink the neighborhood so that
this bound is at most half the leading lower bound.
Since $q^a\asymp|w|^{2a}$, there is then a uniform $c>0$ with
\[
 \Levi_{q^a}(\xi,w\nu)\ge c|w|^{2a}(|\xi|^2+|\nu|^2).
\]
Flatness of $h_0|_D$ gives $\varphi_{0,z\bar z}(z,0)=0$.
Taylor's formula and boundedness of the remaining second derivatives
give
\[
 |\varphi_{0,z\bar z}(z,w)|\le C_2|w|,\qquad
 |\varphi_{0,z\bar w}|+|\varphi_{0,w\bar w}|\le C_2.
\]
Using $2|\xi\nu|\le |\xi|^2+|\nu|^2$ and $|w|\le1$, we obtain
\[
 |\Levi_{\varphi_0}(\xi,w\nu)|
 \le C|w|(|\xi|^2+|\nu|^2).
\]
Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cusp-coefficient}
 \Levi_{\varphi_0+\Lambda q^a}(\xi,w\nu)
 \ge (\Lambda c|w|^{2a}-C|w|)(|\xi|^2+|\nu|^2).
\end{equation}
For $a<\tfrac12$, take $\Lambda=1$ and choose $\delta>0$ such
that $C\delta^{1-2a}<c$; the coefficient in
\eqref{eq:cusp-coefficient} is then positive for $0<|w|<\delta$.
For $a=\tfrac12$, take $\Lambda>C/c$.
Every tangent vector off $D$ can be written as $(\xi,w\nu)$,
so this proves strict positivity there.
For $\gamma=2a\le1$, the inequality
\[
 \bigl||w|^\gamma-|w'|^\gamma\bigr|\le |w-w'|^\gamma
\]
and smoothness of $e^{a\beta}$ show that
$q^a=|w|^{2a}e^{a\beta}$ is locally $C^{0,2a}$.
The weights extend continuously across $D$; the removable singularity
theorem for locally bounded psh functions makes this extension psh.
The correction is a global function, so it preserves the metric
transition law.
\end{proof}

\label{sec:global}
Choose a positive metric $h_K$ on $K$ and extend $h_K^{-1}$ on
$\OO_S(Y)|_Y$ to a smooth metric $h_Y$ on $\OO_S(Y)$. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:baseline-metric}
 h_0=h_Y\otimes\pi^*h_K,\qquad
 \theta_0=\Theta_{h_0}(L),\qquad q=\|s_Y\|_{h_Y}^2.
\end{equation}
Then $h_0|_Y$ is flat and $\Theta_{h_Y}|_{TY}=-\Theta_{h_K}<0$.
Choose $a,\Lambda$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:cusp}, and
$0<\varepsilon<Q=\max_Sq$ such that
$\varphi_0+\Lambda q^a$ is psh on $\{q<\varepsilon\}$. Let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:chi}
 \begin{split}
 B&>\max_{\varepsilon\le t\le Q}(\Lambda t^a-\log t),\\
 \chi(0)&=0,\qquad
 \chi(t)=\max\{\Lambda t^a,\log t+B\}\quad(t>0),
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-metric}
 h=h_0e^{-\chi(q)}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:continuous-metric}
The metric $h$ is continuous and semipositive, with locally
$C^{0,2a}$ weights. In particular it is locally Lipschitz for
$a=\tfrac12$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On $S\setminus Y$, Poincar\'e--Lelong gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:log-curvature}
 \theta_0+\ddc\log q=\pi^*\Theta_{h_K}(K)\ge0.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\theta_0=\Theta_{h_Y}+\pi^*\Theta_{h_K}$ and
$\ddc\log q=-\Theta_{h_Y}$ away from $Y$.
On $\{0<q<\varepsilon\}$ a local weight of $h$ is
\[
 \varphi_0+\chi(q)
 =\max\{\varphi_0+\Lambda q^a,\,
          \varphi_0+\log q+B\},
\]
the maximum of two psh functions. The strict inequality defining
$B$ gives $\log t+B>\Lambda t^a$ for
$\varepsilon\le t\le Q$. By continuity this also holds on
$(\varepsilon-\eta,\varepsilon]$ for some $\eta>0$.
Thus every point with $q\ge\varepsilon$ has a neighborhood on
which only the logarithmic branch is active, and
\eqref{eq:log-curvature} proves positivity there.
Finally, $\log t+B\to-\infty$ and $\Lambda t^a\to0$ as
$t\downarrow0$, so only the cusp branch is active near $Y$.
These neighborhoods cover $S$ and prove continuity and
semipositivity. Near $Y$ the weights are $C^{0,2a}$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:cusp}; away from $Y$ they are locally Lipschitz,
since they are maxima of two smooth functions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:minimal}
For $a=\tfrac12$, $T=\theta_0+\ddc\chi(q)$ has locally Lipschitz
potentials and minimal singularities. Indeed, any positive current
$\theta_0+\ddc v\in c_1(L)$ has a global quasi-psh potential $v$,
and compactness gives
\[
 v-\chi(q)\le\sup_Sv-\inf_S\chi(q)<+\infty.
\]
This answers the bounded-potential question of
\cite[Section~4]{Zhang2025} for Koike's modified example.
\end{remark}

\section{Exhaustions and the radius criterion}\label{sec:radius}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:norm}
If $A\to Z$ is a holomorphic line bundle over a compact complex
manifold and $h$ is a continuous semipositive Hermitian metric, then
$\rho(v)=\|v\|_{h^{-1}}^2$ is a continuous psh exhaustion of
$\Tot(A^{-1})$. It inherits the local H\"older or Lipschitz
regularity of $h$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $v=\lambda_i e_i^*$ and $\|e_i\|_h^2=e^{-\varphi_i}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-norm}
 \rho=|\lambda_i|^2e^{\varphi_i}.
\end{equation}
On overlaps, $\lambda_j=g_{ij}\lambda_i$ and
$\varphi_j=\varphi_i-\log|g_{ij}|^2$, hence
$|\lambda_j|^2e^{\varphi_j}=|\lambda_i|^2e^{\varphi_i}$.
This verifies that \eqref{eq:local-norm} defines a global function.
Off the zero section,
\[
 \log\rho=\log|\lambda_i|^2+\varphi_i
\]
is psh, and composition with the convex increasing exponential
preserves this property. The resulting locally bounded psh function
extends across the zero section to its continuous value $0$.

Fix a smooth Hermitian metric $k$ on $A^{-1}$. Compactness of $Z$
and positivity of the ratio of the two fiber norms give constants
$0<m\le M<\infty$ such that
\[
 m\|v\|_k^2\le\rho(v)\le M\|v\|_k^2.
\]
Thus $\{\rho\le c\}$ is a closed subset of the compact disk
bundle $\{\|v\|_k^2\le c/m\}$ for $c\ge0$; it is empty
for $c<0$. The local expression \eqref{eq:local-norm} also proves
the asserted regularity.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Compact-group averaging]\label{lem:average}
If a compact group $G$ acts continuously on a locally compact
Hausdorff space and $F$ is a continuous exhaustion, then
$\overline F(x)=\int_GF(gx)\,dg$ is an exhaustion.
Averaging over a smooth holomorphic compact Lie group action
preserves $C^r$ plurisubharmonicity, $r\in\{2,\infty\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The function $\overline F$ is continuous by continuity of the
action and integration over the compact group. For
$K_c=\{F\le c\}$,
\[
 \{\overline F\le c\}\subset G\cdot K_c.
\]
Indeed, if $x\notin G\cdot K_c$, then $F(gx)>c$ for all $g$;
compactness of $G$ gives $\min_gF(gx)>c$, so
$\overline F(x)>c$. The saturation $G\cdot K_c$ is the continuous
image of the compact set $G\times K_c$. The displayed sublevel is
closed and therefore compact.

For a smooth holomorphic action write $a_g(x)=gx$.
Differentiation under the integral on compact coordinate sets gives
the $C^r$ regularity, and
\[
 \Levi_{\overline F,x}(\xi)
 =\int_G\Levi_{F,gx}\bigl(d a_g(\xi)\bigr)\,dg\ge0.\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Radius criterion]\label{prop:radius}
If $\Tot(A^{-1})$ admits a $C^r$ psh exhaustion, where $A$ is a
holomorphic line bundle over a compact complex manifold and
$r\in\{2,\infty\}$, then $A$ admits a $C^r$ semipositive
Hermitian metric.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $F_0$ be the given exhaustion and define
\[
 F(v)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}F_0(e^{i\theta}v)\,d\theta.
\]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:average}, $F$ is a $C^r$ psh exhaustion.
For a local frame $e_i$ of $A$, define
\[
 U_i(z,t)=F(z,e^{t/2}e_i^*),\qquad
 \Phi_i(z,\zeta)=(z,e^{\zeta/2}e_i^*).
\]
Circle invariance and holomorphicity of $\Phi_i$ give
\[
 F(z,\lambda_i)=U_i(z,t),\quad t=\log|\lambda_i|^2,
 \qquad V_i=F\circ\Phi_i=U_i(z,\operatorname{Re}\zeta).
\]
The function $V_i$ is psh, and
$V_{i,\zeta\bar\zeta}=U_{i,tt}/4\ge0$. For each fixed $z$,
\[
 U_i(z,-\infty)=F(z,0),\qquad U_i(z,+\infty)=+\infty.
\]
The first limit follows by continuity at the zero section. For the
second, the ray $e^{t/2}e_i^*$ leaves every compact subset of the
fiber as $t\to+\infty$, whereas the sublevels of $F$ are compact.
Convexity and the finite limit at $-\infty$ imply $U_{i,t}\ge0$:
if $U_{i,t}(z,t_0)<0$, the supporting-line inequality would force
$U_i(z,t)\to+\infty$ as $t\to-\infty$.
Moreover, if $U_{i,t}(z,t_0)=0$, monotonicity of the derivative gives
$U_{i,t}(z,t)=0$ for $t\le t_0$, and hence
$U_i(z,t_0)=F(z,0)$.

Choose $c>\max_ZF|_Z$. For each $z$, the intermediate value
theorem and the preceding strict-derivative statement give exactly
one solution $t=\sigma_i(z)$ of $U_i(z,t)=c$.
The implicit function theorem makes $\sigma_i$ a $C^r$ function,
with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:implicit-radius}
 U_i(z,\sigma_i(z))=c,\qquad U_{i,t}(z,\sigma_i(z))>0.
\end{equation}
The first derivatives are
\[
 U_{i,z_j}+U_{i,t}\sigma_{i,z_j}=0.
\]
Differentiating this identity with respect to $\bar z_k$ gives
\begin{align*}
 0={}&U_{i,z_j\bar z_k}+U_{i,z_jt}\sigma_{i,\bar z_k}
 +U_{i,t\bar z_k}\sigma_{i,z_j}
 +U_{i,tt}\sigma_{i,z_j}\sigma_{i,\bar z_k}
 +U_{i,t}\sigma_{i,z_j\bar z_k}.
\end{align*}
All derivatives here are evaluated at $(z,\sigma_i(z))$.
Put $b=\partial\sigma_i(\xi)=\sum_j\sigma_{i,z_j}\xi_j$.
The identities
$V_{i,z_j\bar\zeta}=U_{i,z_jt}/2$ and
$V_{i,\zeta\bar\zeta}=U_{i,tt}/4$ give
\[
 \Levi_{V_i}(\xi,2b)
 =\sum_{j,k}U_{i,z_j\bar z_k}\xi_j\bar\xi_k
  +2\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\sum_jU_{i,z_jt}\xi_j\bar b\right)
  +U_{i,tt}|b|^2.
\]
Contracting the twice-differentiated identity with
$\xi_j\bar\xi_k$ therefore yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:radius-Levi}
 0\le\Levi_{V_i}(\xi,2\partial\sigma_i(\xi))
 =-U_{i,t}\Levi_{\sigma_i}(\xi).
\end{equation}
Thus $\psi_i=-\sigma_i$ is psh. If $e_j=g_{ij}e_i$, then
$\lambda_j=g_{ij}\lambda_i$. The same level set $F=c$ has local
squared radii $e^{\sigma_i}$ and $e^{\sigma_j}$, so
\[
 \sigma_j=\sigma_i+\log|g_{ij}|^2,\qquad
 \psi_j=\psi_i-\log|g_{ij}|^2.
\]
Consequently the formulas
$\|e_i\|_h^2=e^{-\psi_i}=e^{\sigma_i}$ agree on overlaps and
define a $C^r$ Hermitian metric on $A$, with
$\Theta_h(A)=\ddc\psi_i\ge0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:criterion}
For $r\in\{2,\infty\}$ and a line bundle $A$ over a compact complex
manifold,
\[
\begin{gathered}
 \Tot(A^{-1})\text{ admits a }C^r\text{ psh exhaustion}
 \\\Longleftrightarrow\\
 A\text{ admits a }C^r\text{ semipositive metric}.
\end{gathered}
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The forward implication is Proposition~\ref{prop:radius}.
Conversely, a $C^r$ semipositive metric gives the $C^r$ exhaustion
\eqref{eq:local-norm} by Lemma~\ref{lem:norm}. For $C^2$ weights,
its Levi form can also be checked directly, including at $\lambda=0$:
\[
 \Levi_{|\lambda|^2e^\varphi}(\xi,\eta)
 =e^\varphi\left(
    |\eta+\lambda\partial\varphi(\xi)|^2
    +|\lambda|^2\Levi_\varphi(\xi)\right)\ge0.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]\label{sec:conclusion}
The total space has the projective compactification
\begin{equation}\label{eq:compactification}
 M=\PP_{\mathrm{lines}}(\OO_S\oplus L^{-1})
 \setminus\PP_{\mathrm{lines}}(L^{-1}),\qquad
 v\longmapsto[(1,v)].
\end{equation}
The inverse map on the displayed complement sends a line
$[(a,b)]$, with $a\ne0$, to $b/a\in L^{-1}$.
Since $S$ is projective and $L$ is algebraic, the projective bundle
in \eqref{eq:compactification} is projective. Its open subset $M$
is therefore a smooth quasi-projective threefold, and restriction
of a projective K\"ahler form makes it K\"ahler. With
$p:M\to S$, the metric of Proposition~\ref{prop:continuous-metric}
gives the exhaustion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:explicit-exhaustion}
 \rho(v)=\|v\|_{h_0^{-1}}^2\exp\bigl(\chi(q(p(v)))\bigr).
\end{equation}
Take $a=\tfrac12$ to obtain local Lipschitz regularity.
A $C^2$ psh exhaustion would give a $C^2$ semipositive metric on $L$
by Proposition~\ref{prop:radius}, contradicting
Proposition~\ref{prop:no-metric}.
\end{proof}

\section{A non-K\"ahler modification over one point}\label{sec:nonkahler}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:compact-modification}
Let $f:X'\to X$ be a proper surjective holomorphic map of nonempty
complex manifolds, biholomorphic outside $f^{-1}(K)$ for some compact
$K\subset X$. For $r\in\{0,2,\infty\}$, $X'$ admits a $C^r$ psh
exhaustion if and only if $X$ does.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $u$ is a $C^r$ psh exhaustion of $X$, then $u\circ f$ is
$C^r$ and psh, and
\[
 \{u\circ f\le a\}=f^{-1}(\{u\le a\})
\]
is compact by properness.

Conversely, let $F$ be a $C^r$ psh exhaustion of $X'$.
Assume $K\ne\varnothing$, choose an open neighborhood
$K\subset U\Subset X$, and take
$c>\max_{f^{-1}(\overline U)}F$. Define
\[
 \chi(t)=
 \begin{cases}
  0,&t\le c,\\[2pt]
  \displaystyle\int_c^t e^{-1/(s-c)}\,ds,&t>c.
 \end{cases}
\]
This function is smooth, convex and nondecreasing, with
$\chi(t)\to+\infty$ as $t\to+\infty$.
Let $j:X\setminus K\to X'\setminus f^{-1}(K)$ be the
holomorphic inverse of $f$. Then
\[
 G=\chi\circ F\circ j\quad\text{on }X\setminus K,
 \qquad G=0\quad\text{on }K
\]
vanishes throughout $U$, because $F<c$ on $f^{-1}(U)$.
It is therefore $C^r$ across $K$. On $X\setminus K$, convex
increasing composition preserves psh; for $r\ge2$ this is the
identity
\[
 \Levi_{\chi\circ F}(\xi)
 =\chi'(F)\Levi_F(\xi)+\chi''(F)|\partial F(\xi)|^2\ge0.
\]
On $U$ the function $G$ is constant, so it is psh on all of $X$.
For each $a\ge0$, choose $b$ such that
$\chi(t)\le a\Rightarrow t\le b$. If $x\notin K$ and
$G(x)\le a$, then $F(j(x))\le b$. Hence
\[
 \{G\le a\}\subset K\cup f(\{F\le b\})
\]
is a closed subset of a compact set and is compact. Negative
sublevels are empty. If $K=\varnothing$, $f$ is a biholomorphism
and the inverse pullback gives the conclusion directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:point-modification}
For every point $x$ of a connected smooth complex threefold $X$,
there is a connected smooth non-K\"ahler threefold $N$ and a proper
modification $\mu:N\to X$ that is biholomorphic over
$X\setminus\{x\}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Blow up $x$:
\[
 b:W=\Bl_xX\to X,\qquad E=b^{-1}(x)\simeq\PP^2.
\]
In $E$, the curves
\[
 A=\{w=0\},\qquad B=\{uv=w^2\}
\]
meet transversely at $p=[1:0:0]$ and $q=[0:1:0]$.
Set $W_p=W\setminus\{q\}$ and $W_q=W\setminus\{p\}$.
On $W_p$ and $W_q$ respectively, form
\[
 \begin{split}
 N_p&=\Bl_{\widetilde{B\cap W_p}}
             \bigl(\Bl_{A\cap W_p}W_p\bigr),\\
 N_q&=\Bl_{\widetilde{A\cap W_q}}
             \bigl(\Bl_{B\cap W_q}W_q\bigr).
 \end{split}
\]
Here a tilde denotes strict transform. Over
$W_p\cap W_q=W\setminus\{p,q\}$ the two centers are disjoint.
The two blowup orders have a canonical isomorphism there, agreeing
with the identity off $A\cup B$. Glue by this isomorphism to
obtain $N$, with maps
\[
 \nu:N\longrightarrow W,\qquad \mu=b\circ\nu:N\longrightarrow X.
\]
All centers, including their strict transforms, are smooth.
Two points of $N$ with distinct images have disjoint neighborhoods
pulled back from $W$. Two points over the same image lie together
in $N_p$ or $N_q$, where they can be separated. Thus $N$ is a
smooth Hausdorff manifold. The map $\nu$ is proper because its
restrictions over the open cover $W_p,W_q$ are compositions of
proper blowups. Hence $\mu$ is proper as well. The two connected
blowup charts overlap, so $N$ is connected. Every center lies in
$E=b^{-1}(x)$; therefore $\mu$ is biholomorphic over
$X\setminus\{x\}$.

We verify the non-K\"ahler obstruction, as in
\cite[Section~6]{Barlet2015} and \cite[Tag~08J1]{StacksHironaka}.
Let $D_A,D_B$ be the exceptional surfaces dominating $A,B$, with
the induced maps
\[
 f_A:D_A\longrightarrow A,\qquad f_B:D_B\longrightarrow B.
\]
They are smooth compact surfaces: near either crossing they are
ruled surfaces or the blowup of a ruled surface at one point, and
they are closed in the proper inverse image of $A\cup B$.
Let $\alpha,\beta\in H_2(N,\R)$ be their general fiber classes.
For any two points $a,a'\in A\simeq\PP^1$, choose a rational
function $r$ with $\operatorname{div}(r)=[a]-[a']$. Then
\[
 \operatorname{div}(r\circ f_A)=f_A^*[a]-f_A^*[a'].
\]
Thus all fiber divisors of $f_A$ are linearly equivalent and
represent $\alpha$; the analogous assertion holds for $f_B$ and
$\beta$.

We compute the special fibers, including their multiplicities.
Near $p$, take coordinates with
$A=\{y=z=0\}$ and $B=\{x=z=0\}$.
The first blowup has a chart
\[
 (x,y,t)\longmapsto(x,y,z=yt).
\]
Its exceptional divisor is $y=0$, and the strict transform of
$B$ is $x=t=0$. A chart of the second blowup is
\[
 (r,y,t)\longmapsto(x=rt,y,t).
\]
On the strict transform $D_A=\{y=0\}$, the map $f_A$ is
$(r,t)\mapsto x=rt$. Its fiber over $p$ is therefore
\[
 \operatorname{div}(rt)=\{r=0\}+\{t=0\}=R_p+T_p.
\]
The closures of these two components are respectively the strict
transform of the first exceptional fiber and the exceptional curve
of its surface blowup. Both are smooth rational curves, and the
factorization $rt$ gives multiplicity one. The second exceptional
surface $D_B$ has fiber $T_p$ over $p$. Interchanging $A$ and $B$
in the same calculation at $q$ gives
\[
 \alpha=[R_p]+[T_p],\quad\beta=[T_p],\qquad
 \beta=[R_q]+[T_q],\quad\alpha=[T_q].
\]
Subtracting the first pair and the second pair yields
$[R_p]=\alpha-\beta$ and $[R_q]=\beta-\alpha$, hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hironaka-zero-cycle}
 [R_p]+[R_q]=0\quad\text{in }H_2(N,\R).
\end{equation}
The relations take place on compact surfaces, so they hold in
ordinary homology even when $N$ is noncompact. A K\"ahler form
would give
\[
 0=\int_{R_p}\omega+\int_{R_q}\omega>0,
\]
a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:nonkahler-threefold}]
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:point-modification} to $M$ at $x$.
The pullback $\rho\circ\mu$ is a locally Lipschitz psh exhaustion:
$\mu$ is locally Lipschitz and proper. A $C^2$ psh exhaustion on
$N$ would descend after truncation by
Proposition~\ref{prop:compact-modification}, contradicting
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
\end{proof}

\section{Surface regularization and boundary criteria}\label{sec:surfaces}
Throughout this section, $X=S\setminus\supp D$, where $S$ is a smooth
compact connected complex surface and $D\ne0$ is effective. A
\emph{divergent collar} is a psh function on a punctured neighborhood
of $\supp D$ tending uniformly to $+\infty$ at the boundary.
An exhaustion of $X$ always has this divergence property.

\subsection{Boundary tools}\label{sec:surface-pair-reduction}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:surface-boundary-tools}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.5em]
\item\label{item:negative-boundary}
If a connected component of $\supp D$ has negative definite
intersection matrix, $X$ has no continuous psh exhaustion.
\item\label{item:collar-gluing}
Smooth divergent psh collars at all connected boundary components
give a smooth psh exhaustion of $X$.
\item\label{item:matrix-alternatives}
For a connected reduced SNC divisor $E=\bigcup E_i$, let $\lambda$
be the largest eigenvalue of $I=(E_i\cdot E_j)$. If $\lambda>0$,
$E$ has a smooth strictly psh divergent collar. If $\lambda=0$,
$I\le0$, $\dim\ker I=1$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:surface-null-boundary}
 A=\sum_i a_iE_i,\qquad a_i\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad
 A\cdot E_i=A^2=0.
\end{equation}
When $S$ is projective, $A$ is nef.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For \textup{(i)}, Grauert's theorem
\cite[\S4, subsection~8(e)]{Grauert1962} contracts the component to
a point $p$ of a normal surface $Z$. Write the contraction as
$q:W\to Z$; its restriction $W\setminus E\to Z\setminus\{p\}$
is a biholomorphism. An exhaustion $\rho$ on $X$ would therefore give
$u=\rho\circ(q|_{W\setminus E})^{-1}$, psh and divergent at $p$.
Choose a finite local projection
\[
 \pi:V\longrightarrow\Delta^2,\qquad \pi^{-1}(0)=\{p\},
 \qquad v(z)=\max_{y\in\pi^{-1}(z)}u(y).
\]
The function $v$ is continuous on $\Delta^2\setminus\{0\}$.
Off the branch locus it is a finite maximum of psh functions.
Properness of $\pi$ gives local boundedness at each remaining point
away from $0$, so removability makes $v$ psh there as well.
Fix $0<r<1$ and set
\[
 M_r=\max_{\{|z_1|\le r,\ |z_2|=r\}}v<\infty.
\]
For $0<|z_1|<r$, the subharmonic maximum principle on the vertical
disc gives $v(z_1,z_2)\le M_r$ for $|z_2|<r$. Continuity gives the
same bound when $z_1=0$ and $z_2\ne0$. Hence $u\le M_r$ near $p$,
contradicting its divergence.

For \textup{(ii)}, choose disjoint neighborhoods $W_E$ of the
boundary components so that each collar $\Phi_E$ is defined on a
neighborhood of $\overline W_E\setminus E$.
Choose $T_E>\sup_{\partial W_E}\Phi_E$ and a smooth convex
nondecreasing $\chi_E$, zero on $(-\infty,T_E]$ and tending to
$+\infty$. Define
\[
 \rho_E(x)=
 \begin{cases}\chi_E(\Phi_E(x)),&x\in W_E\setminus E,\\
 0,&x\notin W_E.
 \end{cases}
\]
The two formulas agree on a neighborhood of $\partial W_E$, and
\[
 \Levi_{\chi_E\circ\Phi_E}(\xi)
 =\chi_E'(\Phi_E)\Levi_{\Phi_E}(\xi)
  +\chi_E''(\Phi_E)|\partial\Phi_E(\xi)|^2\ge0.
\]
Thus $\rho=\sum_E\rho_E$ is smooth and psh. Every sequence leaving
compact subsets of $X$ approaches $D$ along a subsequence, where the
corresponding summand tends to infinity. This proves properness.

For \textup{(iii)}, Perron--Frobenius applied to $I+c\mathrm{Id}$
shows that $\lambda$ is simple and has a strictly positive
eigenvector $v$. When $\lambda>0$, both $v>0$ and $Iv>0$ persist
under small perturbations. Approximate $v$ by a rational vector and
clear denominators to obtain
$A=\sum a_iE_i$ with $a_i\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$ and
$A\cdot E_i>0$ for all $i$.
Thus $\OO_S(A)|_E$ is ample. A projective embedding by a power
gives a Fubini--Study metric on this restriction. Extend its weights
locally to $S$ and patch them relative to a reference metric.
The extensions have positive tangential curvature along $E$;
near a node, lift the embedding sections from $E$ to a coordinate
neighborhood in $S$. Their ratios have differentials spanning the
two-dimensional Zariski cotangent space of the node, and hence the
ambient cotangent space. Their Fubini--Study weights have positive
curvature in both directions. Keep these weights on smaller node
neighborhoods when patching. The other extensions agree along $E$,
so the patched metric retains positive tangential curvature at its
smooth points.

Let $q=\|s_E\|^2$ for a smooth metric on $\OO_S(E)$.
At a smooth point of $E=\{w=0\}$, write $q=|w|^2e^{-\gamma}$.
Then
\[
 \partial\bar\partial q|_E
   =e^{-\gamma}|_E\,dw\wedge d\bar w.
\]
Away from the node neighborhoods the curvature matrix of the
patched metric has the form
$\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\\bar b&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
in tangential and normal directions, with $a\ge\varepsilon>0$.
Adding $Cq$ to its weights replaces $d$ by
$d+Ce^{-\gamma}$ along $E$; compactness permits one $C$ for which
$a(d+Ce^{-\gamma})>|b|^2$. At the nodes $\ddc q=0$, and at other
points of $E$ it is semipositive, so positivity is preserved there.
Continuity now gives a metric $h$ of positive curvature near all
of $E$. On the complement,
\[
 \ddc\bigl(-\log\|s_A\|_h^2\bigr)=\Theta_h(\OO_S(A))>0,
\]
and $a_i>0$ gives uniform divergence. For $\lambda=0$, every other
eigenvalue is negative. Since $I$ has integer entries and rank one
less than its size, its positive kernel has a rational generator;
clearing denominators proves \eqref{eq:surface-null-boundary}.
Effectiveness gives $A\cdot C\ge0$ for every curve $C\not\subset E$,
which proves nefness in the projective case.
\end{proof}

For a boundary resolution $p:\widetilde S\to S$, the restriction
$\widetilde S\setminus p^{-1}(\supp D)\to X$ is a biholomorphism.
Interior blowups preserve both exhaustion properties by
Proposition~\ref{prop:compact-modification}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:surface-nonconstant-function}
A connected noncompact complex surface with a continuous psh
exhaustion and $\OO(X)\ne\C$ has a smooth psh exhaustion.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
V\^{\i}j\^{\i}itu's theorem \cite[Theorem~1.1]{Vajaitu2022} gives
holomorphic convexity. Let $r:X\to Y$ be the proper Remmert
reduction and let $\tau$ be a smooth strictly psh exhaustion of the
Stein space $Y$. Then
\[
 \rho=\tau\circ r,\qquad
 \{\rho\le c\}=r^{-1}(\{\tau\le c\})\Subset X
\]
is a smooth psh exhaustion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{cor:curve-total-space}
For a line bundle $A$ on a compact Riemann surface $B$,
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 \Tot(A^{-1})\text{ continuously weakly complete}\\
 \Longleftrightarrow\quad\deg A\ge0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\\
 \Tot(A^{-1})\text{ smoothly weakly complete}.
 \end{gathered}
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $d=\deg A\ge0$, choose an area form $\omega$ of integral one
and solve $\ddc f=d\omega-\theta_0$, where $\theta_0$ is the
curvature of a reference metric $h_0$. The right-hand side has
integral zero, so the equation is solvable on the compact curve.
The metric $h=e^{-f}h_0$ satisfies
\[
 \Theta_h(A)=\theta_0+\ddc f=d\omega\ge0,
\]
and Lemma~\ref{lem:norm} applies. The map
$v\mapsto\C(1,v)$ embeds $\Tot(A^{-1})$ into
$\PP_{\mathrm{lines}}(\OO_B\oplus A^{-1})$, with complement
$Y_\infty=\PP(A^{-1})$. Its normal bundle is
\[
 N_{Y_\infty/\PP(\OO_B\oplus A^{-1})}
 =\operatorname{Hom}(A^{-1},\OO_B)=A.
\]
If $d<0$, this is a negative-square boundary, contradicting
Lemma~\ref{lem:surface-boundary-tools}\textup{(i)}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Current averaging and regularization}
\label{subsec:divisor-regularization}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:divisor-regularization}
Let $S$ be compact K\"ahler and $D\ne0$ a reduced divisor.
If $X=S\setminus D$ has a continuous psh exhaustion, it has a
smooth one. If $X$ is not a modification of a Stein space, there is
\[
 A=\sum_i a_iD_i,\qquad a_i\in\mathbb Z_{>0},\qquad
 A\cdot D_i=A^2=0,
\]
such that $\OO_S(A)$ has a smooth Hermitian metric with zero
curvature near $D$. Thus the entire boundary has a smooth
pluriharmonic divergent collar.
\end{theorem}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:level-averaging}
Let $(S,\omega)$ be a compact K\"ahler surface and $X\subset S$
a connected noncompact open set with a continuous psh exhaustion
$\rho$. If $X$ is not a modification of a Stein space, there is
a positive $(1,1)$-current $T$ on $S$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:averaged-current-properties}
 \ddc T=0,\qquad \int_S T\wedge\omega=1,\qquad
 \supp T\Subset X,\qquad \nu(T,x)=0\quad(x\in S).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\Sigma_X^0$ be the intersection of the non-strongly-psh loci
of all continuous psh exhaustions. By
\cite[Lemma~2.1]{MongodiSlodkowski2020},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel-halfline}
 (t_0,\infty)\subset\rho(\Sigma_X^0)
\end{equation}
for some $t_0$. For $t>t_0$, no smooth strictly psh function exists
near $K_t=\{\rho=t\}$. Indeed, if $v$ were such a function,
properness permits $\delta>0$ with the compact shell
$\{t-\delta\le\rho\le t+\delta\}$ contained in its domain.
After scaling and adding a constant, arrange $0<v<1$ there. For
$0<\eta<1$, $0<\varepsilon<\eta\delta/2$, set
$g(s)=s+\eta|s-t|$. The function
\[
 \psi=
 \begin{cases}
 \max\{g(\rho),\rho+\varepsilon v\},& |\rho-t|<\delta,\\
 g(\rho),& |\rho-t|\ge\delta
 \end{cases}
\]
is well defined: near $|\rho-t|=\delta$,
$g(\rho)-\rho=\eta|\rho-t|>\varepsilon v$, so the formulas agree.
Both entries of the maximum are psh, and $g$ is convex and
increasing. On $K_t$ the second entry strictly dominates, so
$\psi=\rho+\varepsilon v$ is strongly psh near $K_t$.
Finally $\psi\ge g(\rho)$ makes $\psi$ an exhaustion. This
contradicts $K_t\cap\Sigma_X^0\ne\varnothing$.

Let $\mathcal B_t$ be the Banach space of continuous real
$(1,1)$-forms on $K_t$, with the uniform norm induced by $\omega$.
Consider the subspace and open cone
\[
 V_t=\{\ddc f|_{K_t}:f\in C^\infty(S,\R)\},\qquad
 P_t=\{\eta\in\mathcal B_t:\eta>0\text{ on }K_t\}.
\]
If $V_t\cap P_t$ were nonempty, continuity would make the
corresponding $f$ strictly psh on a neighborhood of $K_t$.
Conversely, such a local function extends to $S$ after multiplication
by a cutoff equal to one near $K_t$. Hahn--Banach separation thus
gives a nonzero continuous functional $\ell_t$ with
\[
 \ell_t|_{V_t}=0,\qquad \ell_t(\eta)\ge0\quad(\eta\ge0).
\]
The second assertion follows by applying the separating inequality
to $\eta+\varepsilon\omega$ and letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.
Moreover $\ell_t(\omega)>0$: otherwise
$-C\omega\le\eta\le C\omega$ would force $\ell_t(\eta)=0$ for
every $\eta$. Define the current explicitly by
\[
 \langle T_t,\eta\rangle
   =\frac{\ell_t(\eta|_{K_t})}{\ell_t(\omega|_{K_t})}.
\]
Its positivity, support, and normalization are immediate; and
$\langle\ddc T_t,f\rangle=\langle T_t,\ddc f\rangle=0$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:level-current}
 T_t\ge0,\qquad \ddc T_t=0,\qquad
 \supp T_t\subset K_t,\qquad \int_S T_t\wedge\omega=1.
\end{equation}

Fix $t_0<a<b$ and choose
\[
 t_{n,j}=a+\frac{j-1/2}{n}(b-a),\qquad
 T_n=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^nT_{t_{n,j}}.
\]
These currents have mass one and support in the fixed compact shell
$K=\rho^{-1}([a,b])\Subset X$. A subsequence converges weakly to a
positive current $T$. Testing against $\omega$, against $\ddc f$,
and against forms supported outside $K$ respectively gives
\[
 \int_S T\wedge\omega=1,\qquad \ddc T=0,
 \qquad\supp T\subset K.
\]

Fix a coordinate ball $B(x,2R)\Subset X$, and put
$\beta=\ddc|z|^2$. Lelong--Jensen monotonicity for positive
$dd^c$-closed currents \cite[Appendix~B, (B.1)--(B.2)]{DNS22} gives,
uniformly in $t$, for $0<r<R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-lelong-bound}
 r^{-2}\int_{B(x,r)}T_t\wedge\beta
 \le R^{-2}\int_{B(x,R)}T_t\wedge\beta\le C_R.
\end{equation}
Indeed, $\beta\le c_R\omega$ on $B(x,R)$, so one may take
$C_R=c_R/R^2$, independently of $t$. Put
$m_r=\inf_{B(x,r)}\rho$ and $M_r=\sup_{B(x,r)}\rho$.
Since $\supp T_t\subset\{\rho=t\}$, only indices with
$t_{n,j}\in[m_r,M_r]$ contribute. Their number $N_n(r)$ satisfies
\[
 N_n(r)\le\frac{n(M_r-m_r)}{b-a}+2.
\]
Summing \eqref{eq:uniform-lelong-bound} over these indices gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grid-oscillation-bound}
 r^{-2}\int_{B(x,r)}T_n\wedge\beta
 \le C_R\left(\frac{\operatorname{osc}_{B(x,r)}\rho}{b-a}
                  +\frac2n\right).
\end{equation}
Multiply $\beta$ by a cutoff equal to one on $B(x,R)$. The
corresponding positive measures $\mu_n$ converge weakly to $\mu$.
For the open ball, the Portmanteau inequality and
\eqref{eq:grid-oscillation-bound} give
\[
 \mu(B(x,r))\le\liminf_n\mu_n(B(x,r))
 \le\frac{C_Rr^2}{b-a}(M_r-m_r).
\]
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:limit-oscillation-bound}
 r^{-2}\int_{B(x,r)}T\wedge\beta
 \le\frac{C_R}{b-a}\operatorname{osc}_{B(x,r)}\rho
 \longrightarrow0\qquad(r\downarrow0).
\end{equation}
Thus $\nu(T,x)=0$ on $X$; outside $X$, $T$ vanishes locally.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:surface-current-inequality}
For a positive $dd^c$-closed $(1,1)$-current $T$ on a compact
K\"ahler surface with all Lelong numbers zero, there is a class
$\alpha_T\in H^{1,1}(S,\R)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:current-class-pairing}
 \alpha_T\cdot[\eta]=\int_S T\wedge\eta
 \qquad(d\eta=0),
\end{equation}
for every smooth real $(1,1)$-form $\eta$. Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DNS-inequality}
 \alpha_T^2\ge0,\qquad dT\ne0\ \Longrightarrow\ \alpha_T^2>0.
\end{equation}
If $dT=0$, then $\alpha_T=[T]$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The decomposition \cite[\S2.1 and Lemma~B.2]{DNS22}
\[
 T=\Omega+\partial R+\bar\partial\overline R
                  +i\partial\bar\partial u
\]
has $\Omega$ smooth, real, and closed, $R$ of bidegree $(0,1)$,
and $u$ a real distribution. For $d\eta=0$, its pure bidegree
implies $\partial\eta=\bar\partial\eta=0$. Thus
\[
 \langle\partial R,\eta\rangle
 =\langle\bar\partial\overline R,\eta\rangle
 =\langle i\partial\bar\partial u,\eta\rangle=0,
\]
which proves \eqref{eq:current-class-pairing} with
$\alpha_T=[\Omega]$. The nondegenerate intersection pairing on
$H^{1,1}(S,\R)$ makes the class independent of the decomposition.
The density formula \cite[Theorem~2.2(2), Corollary~2.4]{DNS22} gives
\[
 \alpha_T^2=\|\vartheta\|+2E(T),\qquad
 \|\vartheta\|\ge0,\quad E(T)\ge0,
 \quad E(T)=0\Longleftrightarrow dT=0.
\]
Here $\vartheta$ is the positive measure on the diagonal determined
by a tangent current of $T\otimes T$, and
$E(T)=\int_S\bar\partial R\wedge\partial\overline R$ is the
current energy, independent of the chosen decomposition.
The mass hypotheses hold because the positive-Lelong-number locus
is empty. This proves
\eqref{eq:DNS-inequality}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:divisor-regularization}]
If $r:X\to Y$ is a proper modification of a Stein space, the
composition of $r$ with a smooth strictly psh exhaustion of $Y$
is the required exhaustion. Otherwise the preceding lemmas give $T$, supported
in $K\Subset X$, and $\alpha=\alpha_T$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha-basic}
 \alpha\cdot[\omega]=1,\qquad \alpha^2\ge0,
 \qquad dT\ne0\Longrightarrow\alpha^2>0.
\end{equation}
For each component $D_i$, choose a metric of curvature $\theta_i$
and canonical section $s_i$. Choose $\chi\in C_c^\infty(X)$ equal
to one near $K$, and extend
$f_i=\chi\log\|s_i\|^2$ by zero to $S$.
Since $s_i$ is nonvanishing near $K$, Poincar\'e--Lelong gives
$\ddc f_i=-\theta_i$ there. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alpha-boundary-orthogonality}
 \alpha\cdot[D_i]=\int_S T\wedge\theta_i
          =-\langle T,\ddc f_i\rangle=0.
\end{equation}

For a connected boundary component $E$, let $\lambda_E$ be the
largest eigenvalue of its intersection matrix $I_E$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:surface-boundary-tools}\textup{(i)} excludes
$\lambda_E<0$. If $\lambda_E>0$, rational approximation gives an
integral divisor $B=\sum b_iD_i$, with all $b_i>0$ and
$B\cdot D_i>0$ on $E$. In particular,
$B^2=\sum b_i(B\cdot D_i)>0$.
Then $\alpha\cdot[B]=0$, and Hodge index forces $\alpha^2<0$,
contrary to \eqref{eq:alpha-basic}. Therefore $\lambda_E=0$.
Perron--Frobenius gives a one-dimensional positive rational kernel
of $I_E$. Choose a positive integral generator for each $E$ and sum:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:full-support-null-divisor}
 A=\sum_i a_iD_i,\qquad a_i>0,\qquad
 \supp A=D,\qquad A\cdot D_i=A^2=0.
\end{equation}
Here distinct irreducible curves have nonnegative intersection, and
connectedness makes each matrix irreducible; these are the only
properties of the components used in the matrix argument.

Set $c=(\int_A\omega)^{-1}$ and $v=\alpha-c[A]$.
Effectiveness gives $\int_A\omega>0$, while
\eqref{eq:alpha-basic}--\eqref{eq:alpha-boundary-orthogonality} give
\[
 v\cdot[\omega]=0,\qquad v\cdot[A]=0,\qquad v^2=\alpha^2\ge0.
\]
Hodge index is negative definite on $[\omega]^\perp$, so $v=0$
and $\alpha^2=0$. The strict inequality in
\eqref{eq:alpha-basic} then forces $dT=0$. We have proved
\begin{equation}\label{eq:closed-current-null-class}
 dT=0,\qquad [T]=\alpha=c[A],\qquad
 c=\left(\int_A\omega\right)^{-1}>0.
\end{equation}
The $dd^c$-lemma for currents now supplies a real distribution $u$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:global-log-potential}
 \ddc u=T-c[A].
\end{equation}
Choose a neighborhood $U$ of $D$ disjoint from $K$.
On a trivializing chart $U_i\subset U$, write
$s_A=f_i e_i$. Poincar\'e--Lelong and
\eqref{eq:global-log-potential} give the distributional identity
\[
 \ddc\bigl(u+c\log|f_i|^2\bigr)
 =-c[A]+c[A]=0.
\]
Elliptic regularity
for pluriharmonic distributions yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-log-collar}
 u=-c\log|f_i|^2+h_i,
 \qquad h_i\text{ smooth and pluriharmonic on }U_i.
\end{equation}
Indeed, taking a local trace gives a harmonic distribution, hence a
smooth function, and the original $\partial\bar\partial$ equation
then makes it pluriharmonic. The functions $-c\log|f_i|^2+h_i$
agree on overlaps off $D$, since they represent the same distribution $u$.
They therefore define a smooth pluriharmonic function on
$U\setminus D$. In a finite relatively compact chart cover of $D$,
each $h_i$ is bounded and $f_i$ tends to zero along $D$. Since
every $a_i>0$, this proves uniform divergence, including at singular
points of the boundary.
Lemma~\ref{lem:surface-boundary-tools}\textup{(ii)} gives the exhaustion.

For the metric statement, set
$b_i=u/c+\log|f_i|^2=h_i/c$ on $U_i$.
If $e_j=g_{ij}e_i$, then $f_j=g_{ij}^{-1}f_i$ and
\[
 b_j=b_i-\log|g_{ij}|^2,\qquad \ddc b_i=0.
\]
Thus $\|e_i\|_{h_{\rm flat}}^2=e^{-b_i}$ defines a smooth flat
Hermitian metric on $\OO_S(A)|_U$, with
$-\log\|s_A\|_{h_{\rm flat}}^2=u/c$.
For a global smooth metric $h_0$ with weights $b_i^0$, the
differences $b_i-b_i^0$ define a single smooth function $b$ on $U$.
Choose a cutoff $\chi$ supported in $U$ and equal to one on a
smaller neighborhood of $D$. The global metric
$h=e^{-\chi b}h_0$ agrees with $h_{\rm flat}$ there, proving the
claimed curvature statement.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:quasiprojective-regularization}
Every smooth complex quasi-projective surface with a continuous
psh exhaustion has a smooth psh exhaustion.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Resolve a projective closure and principalize the complement ideal,
with centers outside $X$ \cite[Theorems~1.0.1--1.0.2]{Wlodarczyk05}.
The resulting morphism $p:S\to\overline X$ is an isomorphism over
$X$, and $D=(p^{-1}(\overline X\setminus X))_{\rm red}$ is SNC.
Thus $p$ identifies $S\setminus D$ with $X$, including when the
original boundary had isolated points. Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:divisor-regularization} to each noncompact component.
Compact components need only constant functions, and there are
finitely many components.
\end{proof}

The case $\OO(X)=\C$ occurs: take $X=\Tot(A^{-1})$ for non-torsion
$A\in\operatorname{Pic}^0(B)$. Its squared unitary-flat norm is a
smooth psh exhaustion. The degree-$n$ fiberwise Taylor coefficient
of $f\in\OO(X)$ lies in $H^0(B,A^n)=0$ for $n>0$; the constant
coefficient is constant on $B$. Thus $\OO(X)=\C$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:square-zero-continuous-collar}
Let $C\subset S$ be a smooth connected square-zero curve in a compact
K\"ahler surface. A continuous divergent psh collar at $C$ implies
a smooth psh exhaustion of $S\setminus C$. If the complement is not
a Stein modification, $\OO_S(C)$ is unitary flat near $C$ and admits
a pluriharmonic divergent collar.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Let $\Phi$ be the collar, choose a neighborhood $W$ of $C$ such that
$\Phi$ is defined near $\overline W\setminus C$,
and take $M>\sup_{\partial W}\Phi$. Set
\[
 \rho(x)=
 \begin{cases}\max\{\Phi(x),M\},&x\in W\setminus C,\\
 M,&x\notin W.
 \end{cases}
\]
The formulas agree near $\partial W$, and divergence at $C$ makes
$\rho$ a continuous psh exhaustion. In
Theorem~\ref{thm:divisor-regularization}, the single-component kernel
generator can be chosen to be $1$, so $A=C$.
\end{proof}

For reducible $D$, the flat metric is on the weighted bundle
$\OO_S(A)$; the kernel multiplicities need not all equal one.

\subsection{Numerical existence criteria}\label{sec:surface-nonprojective}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:nonprojective}
For a nonprojective $S$ and an effective $D\ne0$, the following
are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.5em]
\item $S\setminus\supp D$ is continuously weakly complete;
\item $S\setminus\supp D$ is smoothly weakly complete;
\item no connected boundary component has negative definite
intersection matrix.
\end{enumerate}
The Hopf case uses \cite[Theorem~1.6]{CFSTZ2026}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Only \textup{(iii)}$\Rightarrow$\textup{(ii)} requires proof.
The classification facts used below are recalled in
\cite[\S2]{FN2005}. If $a(S)=1$, the algebraic reduction is a
holomorphic elliptic fibration $f:S\to B$ with connected fibers,
and every curve is vertical. For a fiber $F=\sum m_iC_i$,
the identities $F\cdot C_i=0$ imply
$m_iC_i^2=-\sum_{j\ne i}m_j(C_i\cdot C_j)$. Substitution gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:surface-fiber-intersection-identity}
 x^{\mathsf T}Ix=-\sum_{i<j}(C_i\cdot C_j)m_im_j
       \left(\frac{x_i}{m_i}-\frac{x_j}{m_j}\right)^2,
 \qquad I=(C_i\cdot C_j).
\end{equation}
The right-hand side vanishes precisely when all $x_i/m_i$ are
equal, since the fiber is connected. Extend a vector on a proper
subset of the components by zero; equality then forces the entire
vector to vanish. Thus every proper principal submatrix is negative
definite. Condition \textup{(iii)} implies that the boundary contains
each fiber support it meets, and
$X=f^{-1}(B\setminus P)$ for a finite nonempty $P$.
For a smooth subharmonic exhaustion $\tau$ of $B\setminus P$,
$\tau\circ f$ is psh and
$\{\tau\circ f\le c\}=f^{-1}(\{\tau\le c\})$ is compact.

Suppose $a(S)=0$. By \cite[Lemma~2.1]{FN2005}, $c_1(L)^2\le0$
for every line bundle $L$. Perron--Frobenius and \textup{(iii)}
therefore give an effective $A$ of full boundary support with
$A^2=0$. Indeed, a positive largest eigenvalue would give an
effective divisor of positive square, and a negative one is excluded
by \textup{(iii)}. At a blowdown $q:T\to T'$ with exceptional curve
$E$, put $A'=q_*A$ and write $A=q^*A'+bE$. Since
$q^*A'\cdot E=0$ and $E^2=-1$,
\[
 0=A^2=(A')^2-b^2,\qquad (A')^2\le0,
\]
so $b=0$ and $A=q^*A'$. In particular $A'$ cannot vanish unless
$A=0$. Iterating to the minimal model $p:S\to S_0$ gives
$A=p^*A_0\ne0$, $A_0^2=0$, and
$\supp A=p^{-1}(\supp A_0)$. The restriction
\[
 p:S\setminus\supp A\longrightarrow S_0\setminus\supp A_0
\]
is proper because the deleted set is the full inverse image of
$\supp A_0$. Pullback therefore preserves an exhaustion.

If $S_0$ is K\"ahler, it is a torus or K3 of algebraic dimension
zero. On a torus, translating an irreducible curve shows its square
is nonnegative, hence zero. Adjunction then gives arithmetic genus
one. A rational normalization is impossible, since any map from
$\PP^1$ lifts to $\C^2$ and is constant. The curve is therefore a
smooth elliptic curve, and a translate is an elliptic subtorus.
The quotient onto the one-dimensional torus gives a nonconstant
meromorphic function. On a K3, $h^0(\OO(-A_0))=0$ because $A_0$
is effective and nonzero, while Riemann--Roch and Serre duality give
\[
 h^0(\OO(A_0))-h^1(\OO(A_0))
 =2+\tfrac12 A_0^2=2.
\]
Two independent sections have a nonconstant meromorphic ratio.
Both possibilities contradict $a(S_0)=0$.

Otherwise $S_0$ is of class~VII. If $b_2(S_0)=n>0$, Enoki's
square-zero-divisor theorem \cite[Main Theorem, p.~454]{Enoki}
makes $A_0$ a multiple of the distinguished cycle of an Enoki surface.
Its complement is an affine-line bundle of degree $-n$ over an
elliptic curve \cite[(0.4), p.~453]{Enoki}. The infinity section of
its $\PP^1$-bundle completion has square $n>0$, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:surface-boundary-tools} gives an exhaustion.
If $b_2(S_0)=0$, a minimal class~VII surface containing a curve is
elliptic or Hopf \cite[p.~453]{Enoki}; $a(S_0)=0$ leaves Hopf.
Since $b_2=0$, every curve has square zero and distinct curves have
intersection zero. Adjunction gives arithmetic genus one, while
rational normalizations are impossible because maps
$\PP^1\to S_0$ lift to $\C^2\setminus\{0\}$ and are constant.
Thus the curves are disjoint smooth elliptic curves.
Finite Ueda type gives a smooth strictly psh collar
\cite[Theorem~2.5]{CLPT2018}. Infinite type gives a unitary flat
neighborhood by \cite[Theorem~1.6]{CFSTZ2026}, hence a
pluriharmonic collar. Glue the collars.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{cor:ruled-all-degrees}
Let $f:S\to B$ be a smooth $\PP^1$-bundle over a compact Riemann
surface and $D\ne0$ reduced SNC. The three conditions in
Theorem~\ref{thm:nonprojective} are equivalent, with no restriction
on $D\cdot F$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Necessity and positive-eigenvalue boundary components are handled
by Lemma~\ref{lem:surface-boundary-tools}. For a connected component
$E$ with zero largest eigenvalue, choose its positive kernel divisor
$A$. If $E$ is vertical, it is a whole fiber, and the punctured-base
construction applies. Otherwise $a=A\cdot F>0$. A vertical
component would be a fiber and have intersection $a$ with $A$,
contradicting $A\cdot E_i=0$. Thus every $E_i$ is horizontal.
The ruling is projective; choose a section $C_0$ with $C_0^2=-e$.
Write
\[
 A\equiv aC_0+bF,\qquad E_i\equiv d_iC_0+b_iF,
 \qquad d_i=E_i\cdot F>0.
\]
Using $C_0\cdot F=1$ and $F^2=0$, compute
\[
 0=A^2=-ea^2+2ab,\qquad
 0=A\cdot E_i=a b_i+d_i b-ea d_i.
\]
Since $a>0$, these identities give
\[
 b=ae/2,\qquad E_i\equiv d_i(C_0+(e/2)F),\qquad
 E_i^2=E_i\cdot E_j=0\quad(i\ne j).
\]
Distinct components are therefore disjoint, and connectedness makes
$E=C$ a single curve, smooth by the SNC hypothesis. For
$d=C\cdot F$, the canonical class formula
\[
 K_S\equiv-2C_0+(2g(B)-2-e)F
\]
and adjunction give $2g(C)-2=(K_S+C)\cdot C=d(2g(B)-2)$.
The nonconstant map $f|_C:C\to B$ is finite of degree $d$.
Riemann--Hurwitz now gives degree zero for its effective ramification
divisor, so it is \'etale.

For $d=1$, the complement is an affine-line bundle with degree-zero
linear bundle. In unitary flat frames, initially write the affine
transitions as $w_i=a_{ij}w_j+b_{ij}(z)$ with $|a_{ij}|=1$.
The construction of \cite[Proposition~2.1]{KoikeUeda2025} gives
pluriharmonic functions $h_i$ with
$h_i=a_{ij}h_j+b_{ij}$. On simply connected charts split
$h_i=f_i+g_i$, with $f_i$ holomorphic and $g_i$ antiholomorphic.
The coordinate changes $w_i\mapsto w_i-f_i$ replace $b_{ij}$ by
\[
 c_{ij}=b_{ij}+a_{ij}f_j-f_i=g_i-a_{ij}g_j.
\]
This expression is both holomorphic and antiholomorphic, hence
constant. In the new coordinates,
\[
 w_i=a_{ij}w_j+c_{ij},\qquad g_i=a_{ij}g_j+c_{ij},
 \qquad |a_{ij}|=1.
\]
The identity $w_i-g_i=a_{ij}(w_j-g_j)$ makes
$\rho=|w_i-g_i|^2$ global. Since $g_i$ is antiholomorphic,
\[
 \rho_{z\bar z}=|(g_i)_{\bar z}|^2,\qquad
 \rho_{z\bar w_i}=\rho_{w_i\bar z}=0,\qquad
 \rho_{w_i\bar w_i}=1.
\]
Thus $\rho$ is smooth and psh. On a finite relatively compact
trivializing cover of the compact base, the $g_i$ are bounded, so
$\rho\le c$ bounds each fiber coordinate. Its sublevels are compact.

For $d\ge2$, let $B'\to B$ be the finite \'etale Galois cover
associated to the kernel of the permutation monodromy
$\pi_1(B)\to\mathfrak S_d$ of $C\to B$. Form
\[
 S'=S\times_B B',\qquad p:S'\to S,\qquad
 C'=p^{-1}(C)=C_1\sqcup\cdots\sqcup C_d.
\]
The $C_i$ are sections with square zero: their normal bundles are
pullbacks of $N_{C/S}$ along finite covers. If $d=2$, these sections
identify the ruling with $\PP(\OO_{B'}\oplus L)$, where
$\deg L=0$, and $S'\setminus C'=L^\times$. For a unitary flat
metric, put $t(v)=\log\|v\|^2$. Then $\ddc t=0$ on $L^\times$,
and
\[
 \Levi_{t^2}(\xi)=2|\partial t(\xi)|^2\ge0,\qquad
 \{t^2\le c\}=\{e^{-\sqrt c}\le\|v\|^2\le e^{\sqrt c}\}
\]
is compact. Hence $t^2$ is a smooth psh exhaustion.

If $d\ge3$, the unique fiberwise M\"obius map sending
$C_1,C_2,C_3$ to $0,1,\infty$ identifies $S'$ with
$B'\times\PP^1$. Every remaining section is a holomorphic map
$B'\to\PP^1\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}$; it is constant by the
maximum principle. Thus $S'\setminus C'=B'\times(\PP^1\setminus Q)$
for a finite set $Q$, and an exhaustion of the punctured fiber pulls
back to one of the product.

In either case let $\rho'\ge0$ be the exhaustion and $G$ the deck
group. The function
\[
 \widehat\rho=\frac1{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\rho'\circ g
\]
is smooth, psh, and $G$-invariant. Since
$\widehat\rho\ge\rho'/|G|$, its sublevels are compact.
It descends under the finite \'etale map $p$ to a smooth psh
exhaustion of $S\setminus C$. Restrict this function to a collar
at $E$ and apply Lemma~\ref{lem:surface-boundary-tools}\textup{(ii)}
to glue all boundary collars.
\end{proof}

\begin{samepage}
\subsection{Square-zero curves}\label{subsec:smooth-boundary}
For a smooth square-zero curve, infinite Ueda type is a formal
condition. A unitary flat neighborhood requires convergent defining
functions $w_i=t_{ij}w_j$, $|t_{ij}|=1$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:surface-ueda-sufficient}
Let $C\subset S$ be smooth, connected, and square-zero, with $S$
compact K\"ahler. Put $N=N_{C/S}$, let
$\rho:H_1(C,\mathbb Z)\to\Uone$ be its unitary character, and set
$K=\ker[H_1(C,\mathbb Z)\to H_1(S,\mathbb Z)]$.
The complement $S\setminus C$ has a smooth psh exhaustion under
any of the following conditions:
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 C\text{ has finite Ueda type or a unitary flat neighborhood};\\
 N\text{ is torsion or Diophantine};\qquad \rho(K)\text{ is finite};\\
 q(S)\ge g(C);\qquad g(C)=1.
 \end{gathered}
\]
Here Diophantine means
$\operatorname{dist}(N^m,\OO_C)\ge c m^{-\tau}$ for some
$c,\tau>0$ and all $m\ge1$. Only the elliptic-curve condition uses
\cite[Theorem~1.6]{CFSTZ2026}.
\end{proposition}
\end{samepage}
\begin{proof}
Finite type gives a smooth strictly psh collar
\cite[Theorem~2.5]{CLPT2018}. In a unitary flat neighborhood,
$w_i=t_{ij}w_j$ with $|t_{ij}|=1$, so the functions
$-\log|w_i|^2$ agree and form a pluriharmonic divergent collar.
In infinite type, Ueda's convergence theorem
\cite[Theorem~3]{Ueda1983} supplies such a neighborhood when $N$
is torsion or Diophantine.

Suppose $\rho(K)$ is finite, and let $k$ annihilate this finite
group. Write $j_*:H_1(C,\mathbb Z)\to H_1(S,\mathbb Z)$ for
the inclusion map. The formula
\[
 \rho_0(j_*\gamma)=\rho(\gamma)^k
\]
defines a character on $\operatorname{im}j_*$, because two lifts
differ by an element of $K$. Divisibility of $\Uone$ extends it
to a character $\widetilde\rho:H_1(S,\mathbb Z)\to\Uone$, with
$\widetilde\rho\circ j_*=\rho^k$. For non-torsion $N$ of infinite
type, \cite[Theorem~4.4]{CLPT2018} then gives an effective divisor
$E$ disjoint from $C$ with $[E]=m[C]$ for some $m>0$.
The line bundle $\OO_S(mC-E)$ has zero real Chern class and hence
a unitary flat metric on the compact K\"ahler surface. Its canonical
meromorphic section $\sigma=s_C^m/s_E$ satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{div}(\sigma)=mC-E,\qquad
 \ddc\log\|\sigma\|^2=m[C]-[E].
\]
On a neighborhood of $C$ disjoint from $E$, the function
$-m^{-1}\log\|\sigma\|^2$ is therefore pluriharmonic off $C$ and
equals $-\log|w|^2$ plus a smooth function. It is the required
divergent collar.

For $q(S)\ge g(C)$, if
$r:H^1(S,\OO_S)\to H^1(C,\OO_C)$ is not injective,
\cite[Proposition~2.7]{CLPT2018} makes a multiple of $C$ a fiber,
and the punctured-base construction applies. Otherwise $r$ is an
isomorphism: its domain has dimension $q(S)\ge g(C)$, and
injectivity forces equality. Hodge decomposition and complex
conjugation then give an isomorphism
$H^1(S,\mathbb Q)\to H^1(C,\mathbb Q)$, whose dual is
$j_*\otimes\mathbb Q$. Consequently $K\otimes\mathbb Q=0$.
As a subgroup of the free abelian group $H_1(C,\mathbb Z)$, $K$
is torsion-free, hence $K=0$, and the preceding character argument
applies.
Finally, if $g(C)=1$, \cite[Theorem~1.6]{CFSTZ2026} rules out
infinite type without a unitary flat neighborhood. Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:surface-boundary-tools}\textup{(ii)} in all cases.
\end{proof}

\section{Holomorphic convexity for curve fiberings}\label{sec:curve-fibers}
Complex spaces in this section are reduced, Hausdorff, and countable at
infinity; smooth functions are understood in local embeddings. Fiber
components refer to reduced supports.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:curve-fiber-convexity}
Let $X$ have pure dimension $n\ge2$ and admit a continuous real-valued
psh exhaustion $\rho$. If a holomorphic map
\[
 F:X\longrightarrow\C^{n-1}
 \quad\text{satisfies}\quad
 \dim F^{-1}(z)\le1\qquad(z\in F(X)),
\]
then $X$ is holomorphically convex and admits a smooth psh exhaustion.
\end{theorem}

For smooth $X$, the convexity argument follows
\cite[Corollary~4.2 and pp.~463--464]{VijituForum2022}.
We establish the required stability on a singular $X$ by using fiber
cycles and a uniform area bound.

\subsection{Compact fiber components}
Write $F=(f_1,\ldots,f_{n-1})$. On each local irreducible branch of
$X$ at $x$, the fiber through $x$ is defined by the $n-1$ equations
$f_k=f_k(x)$. The analytic dimension theorem therefore gives
\[
 \dim_x F^{-1}(F(x))\ge n-(n-1)=1.
\]
Together with the fiber bound, this proves that every nonempty fiber
is pure one-dimensional, also after restriction to a local branch.
Consider the graph and its projection
\[
 \iota:X\longrightarrow\Gamma_F\subset\C^{n-1}\times X,
 \qquad x\longmapsto(F(x),x),\qquad
 p:\Gamma_F\longrightarrow\C^{n-1}.
\]
The map $\iota$ is an isomorphism and $p\circ\iota=F$. The reduced
graph, with multiplicity one on its components, defines an analytic
family of geometric fiber cycles over the normal base
\cite[Chapter~I, \S1(b), p.~816]{BarletMagnusson1998}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curve-fiber-cycles}
 \mathcal F_z=\sum_jm_j(z)[C_j(z)],\qquad
 m_j(z)\in\mathbb N_{>0},\qquad
 |\mathcal F_z|=F^{-1}(z).
\end{equation}
Empty fibers correspond to the zero cycle. These cycles are locally
finite; their components need not be compact.

\begin{lemma}[Uniform area]\label{curve:lem:area}
Fix a Hermitian metric $h$ on $X$. Given compact $L\subset X$ and
$z_0\in\C^{n-1}$, there are a neighborhood $B$ of $z_0$ and $A>0$
such that
\[
 \int_C h\le A
\]
for every compact irreducible component $C\subset L$ of a fiber over $B$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose $\chi\in C_c^\infty(X)$ with $0\le\chi\le1$ and $\chi=1$
near $L$. The map
\[
 I:\C^{n-1}\longrightarrow\R_{\ge0},\qquad
 I(z)=\int_{\mathcal F_z}\chi h
\]
is continuous by integration of compactly supported continuous forms
on an analytic family of cycles
\cite[Chapter~IV, Proposition~2.3.1]{BarletMagnusson2014}.
The integral is finite because $\chi$ has compact support.
Choose $B$ so that $I(z)<I(z_0)+1$ on $B$ and set $A=I(z_0)+1$.
If $C=C_j(z)\subset L$, its multiplicity in
\eqref{eq:curve-fiber-cycles} is at least one, and positivity gives
\[
 \int_C h=\int_C\chi h
 \le m_j(z)\int_C\chi h
 \le\sum_km_k(z)\int_{C_k(z)}\chi h=I(z)\le A.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Stability]\label{curve:lem:stability}
Let $K\subset X$ be compact. If every irreducible component of
$F^{-1}(z_0)$ meeting $K$ is noncompact, the same holds for all
fibers over a neighborhood of $z_0$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Suppose the conclusion fails. There are $z_j\to z_0$, compact
irreducible components $C_j\subset F^{-1}(z_j)$, and
$x_j\in C_j\cap K$. After taking a subsequence, $x_j\to x\in K$.
Let $\nu_j:\widetilde C_j\to C_j$ be the normalization. The function
$\rho\circ\nu_j$ is subharmonic on the compact connected Riemann
surface $\widetilde C_j$, so it is constant. Thus, with
$R=\max_K\rho$,
\[
 \rho|_{C_j}=\rho(x_j)\le R,\qquad
 C_j\subset L:=\{\rho\le R\}\Subset X.
\]
Lemma~\ref{curve:lem:area} bounds $\int_{C_j}h$ for all sufficiently
large $j$. Bishop compactness for cycles on complex spaces
\cite[Theorem~1]{Barlet1999} yields a convergent subsequence
\[
 [C_j]\longrightarrow T\quad\text{in the space of compact
 effective one-cycles of }X.
\]
We verify the support statements used below. First, $x\in|T|$:
otherwise choose an open neighborhood $W$ of $x$ with
$\overline W\cap|T|=\varnothing$. Upper continuity of compact
cycle supports would give $C_j\subset X\setminus\overline W$
for large $j$, whereas $x_j\in W$ for large $j$.
Next, local convergence of integration currents gives $|T|\subset L$,
since every test form supported in $X\setminus L$ has zero integral
on every $C_j$. If $y\notin F^{-1}(z_0)$, choose a neighborhood $W_y$
such that $z_j\notin F(W_y)$ for large $j$. Then
$C_j\cap W_y=\varnothing$, and the same current convergence gives
$|T|\cap W_y=\varnothing$. Consequently,
\[
 x\in|T|\subset L\cap F^{-1}(z_0).
\]
An irreducible component of $|T|$ through $x$ is a compact analytic
curve in the pure one-dimensional fiber $F^{-1}(z_0)$. It is a full
irreducible component of that fiber: a proper analytic subset of an
irreducible curve has dimension zero. This contradicts the hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Convexity and reduction}
We use three complex-space criteria from
\cite[Propositions~2.2, 2.4 and Corollary~2.5]{VijituForum2022}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)},leftmargin=2.5em]
\item A complex space with a continuous psh function $u$ is
holomorphically convex if and only if every $\{u<t\}$ is.
\item A pseudoconvex space locally holomorphically convex over a
Stein base is holomorphically convex.
\item If $\pi:M\to S$ is holomorphic, $S$ is Stein, and
$D\subset M$ is pseudoconvex, then $D$ is holomorphically convex
provided $\overline D^{\,M}\to S$ is proper and a neighborhood
of $\partial_M D$ has Stein reduced fibers.
\end{enumerate}
Here pseudoconvexity means the existence of a continuous psh
exhaustion, and local holomorphic convexity over $S$ means that
every point of $S$ has a neighborhood $V$ whose inverse image is
holomorphically convex.

For criterion~\textup{(iii)}, we record the exhaustion step in the
cited proof. After shrinking a base neighborhood $V\Subset S$, its
finite Stein-neighborhood construction gives smooth functions $h_j$
and nonnegative base cutoffs $\tau_j$ for which
\[
 h_0=\sum_j(\tau_j\circ\pi)h_j,\qquad
 h=h_0+M\phi\circ\pi
\]
is strictly psh on a collar $W$ of the boundary over $\overline V$;
here $\phi$ is strictly psh on the base. To see the role of the last
term, the product rule and the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality on the
finitely many local embeddings give constants $a,b,c>0$ such that
\[
 \Levi_{h_0}(\xi)\ge a\|\xi\|^2-b\|d\pi(\xi)\|^2,
 \qquad
 \Levi_{\phi\circ\pi}(\xi)\ge c\|d\pi(\xi)\|^2.
\]
Taking $Mc\ge b$ gives full strict plurisubharmonicity on the collar.
The functions are smooth on a neighborhood of
$\overline D\cap\pi^{-1}(\overline V)$. Let $\eta_D$ be a continuous
psh exhaustion of $D$ and put
\[
 \psi=\eta_D+h\quad\text{on }D\cap\pi^{-1}(V).
\]
The set
\[
 H=\bigl(\overline D\cap\pi^{-1}(\overline V)\bigr)\setminus W
\]
is a compact subset of $D$. Choose a constant $A$ larger than
$\max_H\psi$ when $H$ is nonempty. Then $\{\psi>A\}\subset W$,
where $\psi$ is strictly psh. For every compact $T\subset V$, the
function $h$ has a lower bound $m_T$ on
$\overline D\cap\pi^{-1}(T)$, and
\[
 \{\psi\le t\}\cap\pi^{-1}(T)
 \subset\{\eta_D\le t-m_T\}\cap\pi^{-1}(T)\Subset D.
\]
The set on the left is closed in the compact set on the right.
Thus $\psi$ is a vertical exhaustion and defines a $1$-convex map.
Theorem~2.3 and Proposition~2.4 of \cite{VijituForum2022} give
criterion~\textup{(iii)}.

\begin{lemma}\label{curve:lem:neighborhood}
For every $\lambda\in\R$ and $z_0\in\C^{n-1}$, there are $c>\lambda$
and a ball $B=B_r(z_0)$ such that
\[
 D=F^{-1}(B)\cap\{\rho<c\}
 \quad\text{and}\quad
 F^{-1}(B)\cap\{\rho<\lambda\}
\]
are holomorphically convex.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $S_0$ be the set of constant $\rho$-values on compact irreducible
components of $F^{-1}(z_0)$. For each $R$, every component whose
value is at most $R$ lies in the compact set $\{\rho\le R\}$.
The locally finite family of irreducible components of the central
fiber has only finitely many members meeting this set. Therefore
\[
 \#\bigl(S_0\cap(-\infty,R]\bigr)<\infty.
\]
Choose $\lambda<a<c<b$ with $[a,b]\cap S_0=\varnothing$ and put
$K=\{a\le\rho\le b\}$. This is compact, and no compact component
of $F^{-1}(z_0)$ meets it. Lemma~\ref{curve:lem:stability} gives a
ball $B=B_r(z_0)$ such that no compact component of a fiber over
$B$ meets $K$. Set
\[
 M=F^{-1}(B),\qquad D=M\cap\{\rho<c\},\qquad
 U=M\cap\{a<\rho<b\}.
\]
Continuity of $\rho$ gives
$\partial_M D\subset\{\rho=c\}\cap M\subset U$.
If a reduced fiber of $F|_U$ had a compact irreducible component $C$,
then $C$ would be closed and analytic in $X$: it is analytic in $U$,
and each point of $X\setminus C$ has a neighborhood disjoint from $C$.
Its dimension would force it to be a full component of the
corresponding $F$-fiber. This is impossible because $C$ meets $K$.
Hence every reduced $U$-fiber is Stein by the criterion for curves
without compact irreducible components
\cite[\S3, p.~1663]{Vajaitu2022}.

On $D$, define
\[
 \eta=\max\left\{\frac1{c-\rho},
                  \frac1{r^2-\|F-z_0\|^2}\right\}.
\]
For $\alpha\in\R$, the function $g_\alpha(t)=(\alpha-t)^{-1}$
on $t<\alpha$ satisfies
\[
 g_\alpha'(t)=(\alpha-t)^{-2}>0,\qquad
 g_\alpha''(t)=2(\alpha-t)^{-3}>0.
\]
Both terms defining $\eta$ are consequently continuous psh functions,
as is their maximum. For $t>0$, the exact sublevel identity is
\[
 \{\eta\le t\}
 =\{\rho\le c-1/t\}\cap
 F^{-1}\{\|z-z_0\|^2\le r^2-1/t\}\Subset D;
\]
for $t\le0$ the sublevel is empty. This proves that $D$ is
pseudoconvex. Furthermore, for compact $T\subset B$,
\[
 \overline D^{\,M}\cap F^{-1}(T)
 \subset\{\rho\le c\}\cap F^{-1}(T).
\]
The left side is closed in the compact set on the right, so
$\overline D^{\,M}\to B$ is proper. Criterion~\textup{(iii)} now
gives holomorphic convexity of $D$. Since $\lambda<c$,
\[
 F^{-1}(B)\cap\{\rho<\lambda\}
 =\{x\in D:\rho(x)<\lambda\},
\]
and criterion~\textup{(i)} gives the second assertion.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:curve-fiber-convexity}]
For $X(\lambda)=\{\rho<\lambda\}$,
Lemma~\ref{curve:lem:neighborhood} makes
$F|_{X(\lambda)}:X(\lambda)\to\C^{n-1}$ locally holomorphically
convex. The continuous psh function
$\eta_\lambda=(\lambda-\rho)^{-1}$ exhausts $X(\lambda)$, since
\[
 \{\eta_\lambda\le t\}=\{\rho\le\lambda-1/t\}
 \Subset X(\lambda)\qquad(t>0).
\]
Criterion~\textup{(ii)} makes every $X(\lambda)$ holomorphically
convex, and criterion~\textup{(i)} then applies to $\rho$ on $X$.
Let $q:X\to Z$ be the proper Remmert reduction onto its Stein
space $Z$, and let $u$ be a smooth strictly psh exhaustion of $Z$.
Holomorphic pullback preserves smoothness in local embeddings and
plurisubharmonicity, while properness gives
\[
 u\circ q\in C^\infty(X)\cap\operatorname{PSH}(X),\qquad
 \{u\circ q\le t\}=q^{-1}(\{u\le t\})\Subset X.\qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Simultaneous contraction]\label{cor:curve-contraction}
The Remmert reduction $q:X\to Z$ has connected fibers,
$q_*\OO_X=\OO_Z$, and $F=\widetilde F\circ q$ for a holomorphic
$\widetilde F:Z\to\C^{n-1}$. Its nontrivial fibers are exactly the
maximal connected unions of compact irreducible fiber curves of $F$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The sheaf identity identifies $\OO(Z)$ with $\OO(X)$ by pullback.
For each coordinate $f_k$ of $F$, let $\widetilde f_k\in\OO(Z)$
be its unique preimage. Then
\[
 \widetilde F=(\widetilde f_1,\ldots,\widetilde f_{n-1}),\qquad
 f_k=\widetilde f_k\circ q,
 \qquad F=\widetilde F\circ q.
\]
Every $q$-fiber is therefore a compact connected analytic subset of
an $F$-fiber. If it is not a point, the dimension bound and local
finiteness make it a finite connected union of compact irreducible
components of that $F$-fiber.

Conversely, $q$ contracts each compact irreducible curve: its image
is a compact analytic subset of the Stein space $Z$, hence is finite
and, by connectedness, a point. Consider a connected union $E$ of
compact irreducible components of one $F$-fiber. The normalization
maximum principle makes $\rho$ constant on each component.
Intersecting components have the same value; local finiteness and
connectedness connect all components by finite chains of intersections.
Thus $\rho|_E=a$ for one $a\in\R$, so $E\subset\{\rho\le a\}$.
Only finitely many fiber components meet this compact set. Hence
$E$ is a finite connected union and $q$ is constant on $E$.
It follows that the maximal such unions are precisely the nontrivial
$q$-fibers.
\end{proof}
