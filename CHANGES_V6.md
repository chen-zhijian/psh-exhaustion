# Changes in Version 6

**21 November 2026** · [Version 6](papers/paper_v6.pdf) · [Version 5](papers/paper_v5.pdf)

## Normal and reduced surfaces

Theorem 1.3 and Corollaries 5.5–5.6 extend the smoothing result to divisor complements in normal compact connected Kähler surfaces and to all normal complex quasi-projective surfaces. The boundary may be an effective Weil divisor. A resolution reduces the assertion to the smooth case. Flattening an exhaustion upstairs near the exceptional fibers allows it to descend smoothly through the finitely many interior singularities.

Remark 5.4 and Proposition 5.7 explain why normality matters. On the complement of an effective Cartier divisor in a reduced irreducible projective surface constructed from a cuspidal cubic, a continuous psh exhaustion exists and every real-valued ambient $C^1$ psh function is constant.

Section 5 retains the boundary criteria, the current-averaging proof and the square-zero collar criterion in Corollary 5.8. Version 5’s separate subsections on numerical existence criteria and square-zero curves are omitted. This manuscript leaves the unrestricted smoothing question for smooth complex surfaces open.

## Dual bundles

Proposition 3.5 characterizes existence of an arbitrary psh exhaustion on the dual total space of a line bundle by existence of locally bounded psh metric weights. Corollaries 3.6–3.7 treat vector bundles of any positive rank over a compact complex manifold. For $E$, the relevant metric is on $\mathcal O(1)$ over the space of lines in $E^*$. The regularity equivalence covers $C^r$ (including continuous and smooth) and local Hölder classes. Arbitrary psh exhaustions correspond to locally bounded psh weights. The real-analytic equivalence is stated for line bundles.

## Holomorphic convexity

Theorem 1.4 removes the continuity hypothesis on the exhaustion from Version 5. A reduced complex space admitting a psh exhaustion is holomorphically convex if it has a holomorphic map to a Stein space for which every irreducible component of each reduced fiber is compact or Stein. The exhaustion may take the value $-\infty$. Section 6 relates the result to Vîjîitu’s criteria and Ohsawa’s surface theorem.
