Outcome
No fatal gap or unsupported enlargement was found in the central divisor-complement regularization theorem. In particular, neither its current averaging lemma nor the DNS application requires a foliation or a smooth exhaustion. The proof is retained with its full hypotheses. This is a mathematical review, not formal verification.

Proof checks
1. The boundary obstruction uses Grauert contraction and boundedness above of a continuous psh function near an isolated puncture of a normal surface. The finite-projection/fiberwise-maximum argument supplies the needed boundedness and does not assume that the given exhaustion extends to the compactifier.
2. The collar gluing construction is valid for finitely many compact boundary components. Boundary blowups require the full inverse-image boundary. No contraction meeting the open surface is used.
3. The positive-eigenvalue collar uses ampleness on the reduced SNC curve, extends positive tangential metrics, treats the full Zariski tangent space at nodes, and adds a squared defining-section norm for the missing normal direction. The zero-eigenvalue kernel is positive and rational by Perron--Frobenius plus integrality.
4. Mongodi--Slodkowski Lemma 2.1 is explicitly stated for arbitrary complex manifolds and CONTINUOUS psh exhaustions. Thus the half-line in the image of the continuous minimal kernel is justified. The proof constructing a strongly psh exhaustion near a level is preserved.
5. Hahn--Banach separates a linear subspace from an OPEN cone; closedness of the subspace is not necessary. The resulting positive order-zero current is dd^c-closed and supported on that level.
6. The discrete averaging proof rules out concentration in its weak limit using a uniform Lelong--Jensen bound and oscillation of the CONTINUOUS exhaustion. The open-set Portmanteau inequality has the correct direction. There is no hidden measurable selection or point-dependent weak subsequence.
7. DNS Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.4 apply to arbitrary positive dd^c-closed currents on compact Kähler surfaces. Their hypotheses hold because the positive-Lelong locus is empty. The density mass formula gives alpha_T^2 >= 2 E(T) >= 0, and E(T)=0 iff dT=0. The cohomology class is defined through the smooth closed part of the current decomposition; no de Rham class is assigned prematurely to a nonclosed current.
8. Orthogonality to every boundary component uses cut-off Poincare--Lelong potentials and dd^c-closedness only. Hodge index excludes positive boundary eigenvalues, then forces the null class and closedness. Positivity of every coefficient of A guarantees divergence at every end, including singular boundary points.
9. The original proof constructs the flat metric on a neighborhood. The replacement explicitly extends it to a global smooth Hermitian metric via a cutoff relative to a reference metric, retaining flatness on a smaller neighborhood. This supplies a routine omitted step in the global metric wording; it does not change the theorem.
10. The quasi-projective reduction uses a smooth projective compactification and principalization outside X, including isolated boundary points. Connected components are finite. The non-torsion degree-zero total-space example really has only constant ANALYTIC holomorphic functions, by its fiberwise Taylor expansion.
11. Nonprojective numerical criterion: checked a=1 verticality and negativity of proper fiber submatrices; checked the blowdown identity 0=(A')^2-b^2 that forces full pullback in a=0; checked the torus/K3 exclusions and the restricted Enoki square-zero-divisor input rather than an unproved complete class-VII classification. The affine bundle has negative linear degree, hence its infinity section has positive square.
12. Smooth ruling criterion: checked the numerical reduction to a single smooth square-zero multisection, adjunction/Riemann--Hurwitz, the affine harmonic-section Levi matrix, and proper finite-group averaging after splitting the multisection by an etale Galois cover.
13. Ueda conditions: finite type, convergent flatness, and formal infinite type remain distinct. The extension of a unitary character from an abelian subgroup uses divisibility of U(1). Neeman's theorem is used only in the non-torsion, infinite-type case. The irregularity argument correctly passes through Hodge theory and torsion-freeness of H_1(C,Z). The 2026 elliptic input is valid for compact COMPLEX surfaces, so its use for Hopf surfaces is within its stated scope.
