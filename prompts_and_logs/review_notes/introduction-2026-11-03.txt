VERSION 3 — ABSTRACT AND INTRODUCTION REVISION
3 November 2026

This record describes an editorial and historical-attribution update to
Version 3. It does not constitute a new mathematical audit or a
proof-assistant certificate. Earlier version PDFs and review records remain
unchanged.

The abstract is replaced by the concise text approved by the authors. An
applications subsection in the introduction explains five connections:
Nakano's smoothing question; Filip–Tosatti's non-semipositivity problem and
Tosatti's bounded-potential question recorded by Zhang; Koike's obstruction;
Ohsawa's and Vîjîitu's holomorphic-convexity results; and the seminegative
line-bundle converse discussed by Ohsawa. Original references are supplied.

Attribution and scope

Nakano's question is Problem 1, page 162, in the 1980 Sugaku collection
edited by Wakabayashi. The unrestricted surface question is not claimed
to be settled. The negative answer extends from dimension three to higher
dimensions by taking a product with affine space; restriction to a closed
slice preserves the obstruction to a C^1 psh exhaustion.

The original non-semipositivity problem is attributed jointly to Filip
and Tosatti. The bounded-potential question is described as suggested by
Tosatti and recorded in Section 4 of Zhang's paper. The constructed
potentials are locally Lipschitz, hence bounded, and have minimal
singularities; their curvature is smooth and strictly positive off the
negative section.

The C^1 strengthening of Koike's obstruction is stated for the Grauert
examples proved in this manuscript. It is not presented as an extension
of every case of Koike's arbitrary-dimensional theorem.

The curve-fibering result is compared with Vîjîitu's argument under the
bound on every nonempty fiber. The singular-space extension is identified
explicitly; no unqualified generalization of his full rank theorem is
claimed. The irreducible surface case is credited to his surface theorem.

Ohsawa's seminegative converse is located after Proposition 6.1 in Section
6 of his RIMS Kokyuroku 2175 article. It is described as a discussed
converse, not a numbered conjecture. The manuscript establishes the linear
line-bundle case over compact complex bases, not arbitrary affine torsors.
